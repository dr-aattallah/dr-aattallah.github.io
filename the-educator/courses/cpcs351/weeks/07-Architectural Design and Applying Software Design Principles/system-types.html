Four Common Types of Systems

Classify the subsystem before choosing how to design it.

4of 14
Learning focus

Distinguish interactive, event-driven, transformational, and object-persistence systems.

Core lecture

Characteristics

  • Interactive: actor-system interaction carries out a business task; requests/responses usually follow a relatively fixed sequence, often one actor at a time.
  • Each interactive request is processed and answered; interaction begins and ends with the actor; the relationship often resembles client-server and system state reflects business-process progress.
  • Event-driven: reactive, state-dependent behavior; events arrive in no fixed order; not every event needs a response and the same event can cause different responses in different states.
  • Event-driven systems may interact with several entities at once, often devices/software, and may have timing, temporal, and timed-temporal constraints.
  • Transformational: a network of information-processing activities transforms input into substantially different output, usually with little actor interaction during processing.
  • Transformations may include sequencing, branching, parallel threads, synchronous/asynchronous behavior, number crunching, and computation-intensive algorithms; they are usually stateless.
  • Object-persistence: provides storage, retrieval, and update of persistent objects; hides storage implementation and performs little business processing beyond performance needs.
  • Persistence must efficiently handle large amounts of structured and complex data.
  • Systems and subsystems are recursive: a subsystem may itself contain subsystems, and one system may contain several subsystem types.
