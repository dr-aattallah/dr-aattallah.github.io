From Requirements to Architecture

Move from defining what the software must do to deciding how its major parts will work together.

2of 14
Learning focus

Explain architectural definitions, importance, abstraction, decomposition, interfaces, interactions, tradeoffs, rationale, and traceability.

Core lecture

Requirements become structure

  • Requirements specify what customers and users want and what the software must do.
  • Architecture specifies how the product will do its tasks by decomposing it into modules or subsystems with interfaces.
  • Architectural design specifies high-level behavior, interactions, and non-functional properties.
  • Key tradeoffs include schedule vs budget, cost vs robustness, fault tolerance vs size, and security vs speed.
  • Design decisions and rationale should be recorded and traceable to requirements.
  • Perry & Wolf: architecture = elements, form, rationale — what, how, why.
  • Shaw & Garlan: elements, interactions, composition patterns, and constraints.
  • Kruchten: high-level structure involving abstraction, decomposition, composition, style, and aesthetics.
  • The architectural design process includes system structuring, control modeling, and modular decomposition.
