CPCS 351 / Topic 06 / Actors & Boundaries
Set the viewpoint first

The boundary decides who is an actor.

An actor is an external business role that interacts with the chosen subject. The same person or system can be inside one boundary and outside another, so identify the system or subsystem before drawing actors.

Ask “external to what?” A university employee may be internal to the university business but external to a software subsystem.

Actor definition

Actors are roles—not job titles copied blindly

Human role

Student, Advisor, Librarian, Customer, Receptionist.

External system

Payment Gateway, University Identity Service, Bank Network.

Device or organization

Sensor, Card Reader, Government Agency, Shipping Provider.

Discovery questions

Find actors from responsibility and value

  • Who initiates this business task?
  • For whom is the task performed?
  • Who receives or acknowledges the useful result?
  • Which external system supplies information or services?
  • Who maintains, administers, audits, or configures the subject?
  • Is this entity still external after we choose a narrower subsystem boundary?
Boundary comparison

One organization, two valid models

Subject: University advising business

Advisor may be a business worker inside the modeled organization; Student is an external customer actor.

Subject: Advising appointment software

Both Student and Advisor are actors because both interact from outside the software boundary.

Common actor errors

Do not confuse roles with implementation

DatabaseUsually an internal implementation detail, not an actor—unless an independently owned external database service interacts with the subject.
Screen or pageAn interface element is not a role seeking value.
“User” everywhereToo vague when distinct roles have different goals or permissions.
A named personActors describe repeatable roles, not individual identities.
Think like a software engineer

Is the bank an actor of an ATM?

Yes when the modeled subject is the ATM subsystem and the bank network is external. The bank provides authorization and account services needed by ATM use cases.

Key takeaway

Define the subject first. Then identify external roles that initiate tasks, support interactions, or receive useful outcomes.
