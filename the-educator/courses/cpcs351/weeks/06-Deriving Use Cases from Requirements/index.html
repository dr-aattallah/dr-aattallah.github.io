Deriving Use Cases from Requirements

Translate requirement statements into complete, actor-centered business goals and prove that the resulting model covers what stakeholders need.

1of 9 pages
Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning focus

Use UML use-case notation and style to depict high-level user requirements and write use-case-centric functional specifications.

Core lecture

What you need to understand

  • A use case is a business process that begins with an actor, ends with the actor, and accomplishes a useful business task.
  • Use cases are derived from requirements and should satisfy those requirements.
  • Derivation identifies actors and system/subsystem scope, extracts candidate verb–noun goals, validates them, specifies them at the right abstraction, and maintains traceability.

Worked example

Library requirement

“The system shall allow a patron to check out documents” → Actor: Patron → Use case: Check Out Document → Subject: Library System.

Remember

Derivation is reasoning from stakeholder statements to complete, traceable actor goals.
