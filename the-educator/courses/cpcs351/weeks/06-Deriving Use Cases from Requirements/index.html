Topic 06 · Goal discovery

Translate requirements into actor goals.

A use case should represent a complete, useful business task for an external actor. Derivation turns requirement statements into a structured model of who needs the system, what outcome they seek, and which requirements each use case satisfies.

Do not turn every verb into a use case. First find candidate goals; then test whether each candidate is complete and valuable for an actor.

Lecture baseline

A use case completes a business task for an actor

Begins with an actor

An external role initiates or triggers the interaction.

Crosses the boundary

The actor and the system exchange information or actions.

Ends with the actor

The actor receives or recognizes the outcome.

Delivers value

The outcome accomplishes a useful business task—not a technical step.

Why this matters

Use cases connect several engineering activities

Requirements

Organize functional capabilities around stakeholder goals.

Planning

Prioritize and schedule coherent slices of business value.

Analysis & design

Reveal responsibilities, collaborations, and subsystem boundaries.

Testing

Provide end-to-end scenarios and observable acceptance outcomes.

Mentor insight

The lecture calls a use case a business process. The practical emphasis is completeness and actor value: a button click, database query, or internal algorithm is normally only a step inside a use case.

Key takeaway

Derivation is a reasoning process: identify actors and scope, extract candidate goals, validate complete business value, choose the right abstraction, and prove coverage through traceability.
