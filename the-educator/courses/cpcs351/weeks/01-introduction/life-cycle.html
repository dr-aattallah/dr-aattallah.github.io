Topic 01 · Section 5

Software Life Cycle Activities

Software engineering is not one activity. It is a coordinated set of life-cycle activities that move a system from idea to operation while managing quality, cost, time, and productivity.

“Think of the life cycle as three tracks running together—not three isolated jobs.”
— Your Software Engineering Mentor

From the Original Lecture

Three interacting life-cycle tracks

The original lecture identifies three interacting tracks: the Software Development Process, Software Quality Assurance, and Software Project Management.

Software Life Cycle

Software Development Process

Transforms the initial system concept into an operational software system.

Software Quality Assurance

Checks that development activities are performed properly and that software artifacts satisfy requirements and quality standards.

Software Project Management

Plans, coordinates, monitors, and controls the development and quality activities.

↓ CostUse resources responsibly
↓ Time to MarketDeliver useful software sooner
↑ QualityMeet requirements and standards
↑ ProductivityDeliver more useful value effectively

Visual Story

One project, three tracks

1. The idea appears

A business need or system concept is identified.

2. Development creates the solution

The team analyzes, designs, implements, tests, and deploys the software.

3. Quality checks the work

SQA verifies that the process is followed correctly and the artifacts meet requirements and quality expectations.

4. Management keeps the project under control

Plans, schedules, risks, people, cost, and progress are continuously monitored.

How They Interact

The tracks are connected

Development ↔ SQA
Developers produce software artifacts; SQA evaluates whether the activities and artifacts satisfy required processes and quality standards.
Development ↔ Management
Development work consumes time and resources; project management plans, schedules, coordinates, and monitors that work.
SQA ↔ Management
Quality findings can reveal defects, risks, rework, or delays that affect project plans and management decisions.

Modern Example

University registration system

Development implements course search, registration rules, prerequisites, APIs, and interfaces. SQA checks that requirements are satisfied and important workflows behave correctly. Project management coordinates the teams, deadlines, risks, and release date before registration opens.

Key Takeaway

Software engineering succeeds when development, quality assurance, and project management operate as coordinated life-cycle activities. Focusing on only one track creates blind spots.

Think Like a Software Engineer

What happens if one track disappears?

Imagine a team with excellent developers but no quality assurance or project management. What problems might appear? Then imagine a project with excellent planning but weak development. Compare the outcomes.
