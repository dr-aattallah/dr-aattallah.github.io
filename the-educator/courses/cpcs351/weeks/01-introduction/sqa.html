Software Quality Assurance

SQA asks two critical questions: Are we performing the development activities properly? And do the resulting software artifacts meet the requirements and desired quality standards?

“Quality is not a final inspection. It must be protected throughout the work.”
— Your Software Engineering Mentor

From the Original Lecture

What does SQA ensure?

⚙️

1. Activities are performed properly

SQA checks whether development work follows the expected processes, procedures, methods, and controls.

✓

2. Artifacts meet requirements and quality standards

SQA checks whether the outputs of development satisfy the required expectations for the software and its artifacts.

Visual Hook

The Quality Gate

Think of SQA as a quality gate that looks both at how the work was done and at what the work produced.

Trusted Artifact

Why This Matters

Quality problems can hide beneath a working interface

A requirement was misunderstood.
A security check was omitted.
A test was never executed.
A design rule was violated.
A change introduced a regression.
Look beyond “it seems to work”

Modern Example

SQA in a university registration system

Quality assurance across the work

More than just testing the final screen

Requirements Review whether prerequisite, seat-capacity, security, privacy, and registration rules are clear, complete, and testable.
Design Check that architecture and interfaces address important requirements such as security, reliability, maintainability, and integration.
Implementation Use reviews, coding standards, static analysis, automated checks, and controlled changes to reduce avoidable defects.
Testing Confirm that required tests exist, are executed, and provide evidence that expected behavior and important quality characteristics are satisfied.
Release Verify that required quality evidence is available before the system is released to students.
Mentor Warning

Testing and SQA are related, but they are not identical. Testing is one important way to evaluate software behavior. SQA has a broader concern: the quality of both the development activities and the artifacts those activities produce.

Key Takeaway

Software Quality Assurance ensures that development activities are performed properly and that the resulting software artifacts meet requirements and desired quality standards.
