Software Process Models

A software process defines the activities needed to construct a software system. A process model organizes those activities into a recognizable development route. Different models handle uncertainty, feedback, risk, and change in different ways.

“The destination may be the same—a working system—but the route can be very different.”

Visual Hook

Five Process Tracks

All five models start with a software-development problem and aim at the same destination: a useful software system. What changes is the shape of the journey—how work, feedback, risk, and delivery are organized.

The Five Models

Recognize each model by its dominant idea

↘
Waterfall Model

Linear sequential progression through separate phases. Easy to understand and manage when requirements are well understood.

🧪
Prototyping Process Model

Build a throwaway working model based on currently known requirements to understand what users really need.

🌱
Evolutionary Development

The prototype is not discarded; it evolves into the production system.

🎯
Spiral Process Model

An iterative model with explicit emphasis on planning, risk analysis, engineering, and evaluation.

⚡
Agile Process Models

Short sprints or iterations develop and deliver predetermined features while responding rapidly to change.

Before Choosing a Model

Ask what kind of uncertainty you are facing

❓
Are requirements clear?

Stable requirements favor different process behavior than unclear or rapidly changing requirements.

🧭
How much feedback is needed?

Some projects need early user feedback before the team can confidently define the final solution.

⚠
How important is risk?

High uncertainty or technical risk may require explicit analysis and reduction activities.

⏱
How quickly must value appear?

Some projects cannot wait until near completion before users see working software.

🔄
How likely is change?

The process must be able to cope with changing requirements and circumstances when change is expected.

👥
How will teams coordinate?

Plan-driven structure, iteration, documentation, and feedback affect how people coordinate their work.

Mentor Insight

A process model is not a badge of maturity. It is an engineering choice about how work, feedback, change, risk, and delivery will be organized.

Key Takeaway

The main process models introduced here are Waterfall, Prototyping, Evolutionary Development, Spiral, and Agile. Each organizes software-development activities differently and responds differently to uncertainty, feedback, risk, and change.

Think Like a Software Engineer

Two projects have the same budget and team size. Must they use the same process model?

Compare their requirement clarity, technology familiarity, system complexity, risk, need for feedback, schedule pressure, and expected amount of change before deciding.
