Topic 04 · Agile Family

Agile Methods

Agile is not one single method. It is a family of approaches sharing an adaptive direction but organizing work differently. Well-known methods include Dynamic Systems Development Method (DSDM), Feature Driven Development (FDD), Scrum, Extreme Programming (XP), Crystal Clear, and Lean Development.

“Same Agile direction, different mechanics. Learn what each method makes especially visible.”

Agile sits at the center; each method emphasizes a different way to organize adaptive software development.

Iterative delivery with strong business involvement and attention to controlled delivery constraints.

HOOK: TIME + BUSINESS

FDD

HOOK: BUILD BY FEATURE

Scrum

A lightweight framework using a Scrum Team, Sprints, events, artifacts, goals, inspection, and adaptation.

HOOK: SPRINT FEEDBACK LOOP
⚙️

Extreme Programming (XP)

Places especially strong attention on software-engineering practices that support rapid feedback and safe change.

HOOK: ENGINEERING DISCIPLINE
💎

Crystal Clear

Emphasizes people, communication, and adapting the way of working to the project context.

HOOK: PEOPLE + CONTEXT
♻️

Lean Development

Focuses on value, learning, flow, and reducing effort that does not contribute useful value.

HOOK: VALUE, LESS WASTE
Worked Example

Scrum applied to the University Registration System

A Sprint can target one coherent outcome instead of attempting the whole system at once.

1

Product Backlog

Seats, prerequisites, conflicts, wait-list, notifications.

2

Sprint Planning

Goal: prevent invalid course registration.

3

Sprint Work

Build prerequisite and conflict validation; inspect progress daily.

4

Sprint Review

Stakeholders inspect the working result and give feedback.

5

Retrospective

Inspect how the team worked and improve the next Sprint.

Increment: students receive immediate prerequisite and schedule-conflict validation during registration.
Method Comparison

Agile ≠ Scrum

The methods belong to the Agile family, but they are not interchangeable names.

MethodMental hookWhat to noticeExample
DSDMTime + businessControlled iterative delivery with active business involvement.Fixed-date admissions portal with continuous business prioritization.
FDDFeaturesProgress decomposed into small client-valued functions.Enroll student; calculate seats; send confirmation.
ScrumSprint loopTeam, events, artifacts, goals, inspection and adaptation.Two-week Sprint delivering conflict checking.
XPEngineering practicesTechnical feedback and disciplined coding support frequent change.Keep registration-rule changes protected by fast feedback.
Crystal ClearPeople + contextTailor working practices to team/project context.Small team building a departmental scheduling tool.
Lean DevelopmentValue + flowReduce activities that do not improve useful value.Remove redundant approval handoffs.
FDD Close-Up

Feature Driven Development in five moves

FDD is memorable because its process can be expressed through five named activities.

1

Develop Overall Model

Understand the domain and establish the overall model.

2

Build Feature List

Decompose the domain into client-valued features.

3

Plan by Feature

Sequence feature sets and assign responsibility.

4

Design by Feature

Create and inspect the design for selected features.

5

Build by Feature

Implement, inspect, unit test, and promote to the build.

Example feature: “Enroll a student in an available section.”
Three Examples

Same product, different Agile viewpoints

Scrum View

Food-delivery app: Sprint Goal = make order tracking useful.

  1. Select tracking backlog items.
  2. Build in the Sprint.
  3. Inspect the Increment.
  4. Adapt what comes next.

FDD View

Describe client-valued features.

  1. Show courier location.
  2. Calculate ETA.
  3. Notify delay.
  4. Confirm delivery.

XP View

Focus on the engineering feedback behind rapid change.

  1. Express expected behavior.
  2. Change code in small steps.
  3. Get fast feedback.
  4. Keep code changeable.
Illustrated Story

“Use Agile” is only the beginning

1. Direction

The team wants adaptive delivery.

2. Question

What needs structure: teamwork, engineering, features, business control, or flow?

3. Method

Scrum, XP, FDD, DSDM, Crystal and Lean emphasize different mechanics.

4. Purpose

Deliver, learn, collaborate and adapt—not merely perform rituals.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Agile is a family of methods. Well-known methods include DSDM, FDD, Scrum, XP, Crystal Clear, and Lean Development. Recognize their shared adaptive direction, then distinguish the emphasis each method brings.

THINK LIKE A SOFTWARE ENGINEER

“We use Agile because we have a Daily Scrum.” Is that enough?

No. Ask what is delivered, how feedback changes decisions, how engineering quality is protected, and whether the chosen method actually helps the team respond to change.
