Process & Methodology

Software development needs both a process and a methodology. The process defines the phases and activities that must happen; the methodology explains how those activities are actually performed.

“Process tells the team where the journey goes. Methodology tells them how to travel each part of the route.”

Visual Hook

Route vs. Playbook

A process gives the overall route: phases, products, dependencies, and milestones. A methodology zooms into a phase and gives the operational playbook for doing the work.

Why Engineering Needs Both

Real projects create coordination pressure

Large development efforts can span years, involve multiple departments and teams, use different tools and processes, and operate across different locations. Requirements, constraints, platforms, and technologies can also change.

DEVELOPMENT
REALITY
Long Development Duration How do we schedule and manage work when customer needs and the future are not fully known?
Many Departments & Teams How do we divide work and later integrate what different groups produce?
Different Processes, Methods & Tools How do distributed teams communicate and coordinate correctly?
Changing Requirements & Constraints How should the process and product be designed so they can cope with change?
Heterogeneous Systems Hardware, software, third-party components, languages, platforms, machines, and locations may all differ.
Core Definitions

Do not confuse the two

Software Process

A software process defines a series of activities performed to construct a software system. Each activity produces artifacts that become input to other phases, and each phase has entrance and exit criteria.

Defines the phases / activities.
Each phase produces products or artifacts.
Outputs feed later phases.
Phase completion establishes milestones.

Methodology

A methodology implements a process—or a phase of a process—by detailing how the work is performed.

Describes detailed steps and subtasks.
Defines inputs, outputs, and representations.
Defines entrance and exit conditions.
Provides procedures, methods, techniques, and flow relationships.

Example Closer to Students

University Registration System

The same project can show the difference clearly: the process gives the major phases, while the methodology tells the team exactly how to perform a selected phase.

SOFTWARE PROCESS · UNIVERSITY REGISTRATION SYSTEM
Requirements Understand registration rules, prerequisites, conflicts, users, and constraints.
Design Define architecture, interfaces, data, responsibilities, and major components.
Implementation Build services, UI, APIs, validation logic, and data access.
Testing Verify prerequisites, seat limits, conflicts, integration, and expected behavior.
Now zoom into the Requirements phase. A methodology would specify the detailed elicitation steps, stakeholder interviews, inputs to each step, requirement representations, entrance/exit criteria, review procedures, techniques, and the flow between those activities.
Illustrated Story

The team that had a process—but no methodology

Panel 1

The process is clear

The team knows it must complete requirements, design, implementation, and testing.

Panel 2

But “how” is undefined

Different analysts document requirements differently and use different criteria for “done.”

Panel 3

Outputs do not fit

Designers receive inconsistent artifacts because the team followed the phase name but not a shared method.

Panel 4

Methodology creates repeatability

Steps, inputs, outputs, criteria, procedures, and techniques make the process executable and consistent.

Mentor Insight

Naming a phase is not enough. “Do requirements” is a process-level instruction. A methodology makes it actionable by defining how requirements work is actually carried out.

Key Takeaway

A software process defines the phases and activities required to construct a software system. A methodology implements the process by detailing how those activities are performed. Software development needs both.

Think Like a Software Engineer

A team says, “Our process includes testing.” What would you ask next to discover whether they actually have a testing methodology?

Ask about steps, inputs, outputs, representations, entrance and exit criteria, procedures, techniques, and how results flow to other development activities.
