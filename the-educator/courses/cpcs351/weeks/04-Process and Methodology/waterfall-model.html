Topic 04 · Model 01

The Waterfall Model

A linear sequential model: each phase receives results from the previous phase and moves the project toward the next milestone. Its greatest strength—structure—is also the source of its greatest weakness—rigidity when reality changes.

“Think of a staircase: decisions and artifacts flow downward. Returning several steps upward can be expensive.”

Strengths vs Limitations

Why structure helps—and why it can hurt

✓ Strengths

✓Suitable for simple or smaller projects with well-understood requirements.
✓Easy to understand and manage.
✓Clear milestones and extensive documentation.
✓Plan-driven coordination can help large systems engineering projects across sites.

⚠ Limitations

!Difficult to accommodate requirement changes.
!Long duration can leave the delivered system outdated.
!No complete working product until near completion.
!Unexpected risks are harder to handle; late failure can waste major investment.
When Does It Fit?

Choose it for its assumptions, not its familiarity

✓ Better fit

Requirements are well understood, change is limited, milestones/documentation matter, and plan-driven coordination is valuable.

✕ Weak fit

Users are still discovering needs, requirements change frequently, early working software is essential, or major risks need experimentation.

Mentor Insight

Waterfall is not “bad software engineering.” The key question is whether its assumptions fit the project. Stable requirements and strong coordination favor it; continuous discovery and change work against it.

Key Takeaway

Waterfall organizes development into distinct sequential phases with clear outputs and milestones. Its clarity helps management, but inflexible partitioning makes changing requirements expensive after work is underway.

Think Like a Software Engineer

A prerequisite rule changes during Acceptance Testing. Which earlier artifacts may now be invalid?

Trace backward: requirements → design → affected code → unit tests → integration tests → acceptance scenarios. That chain explains the cost of late change.
