Agile Process Models

Agile organizes development around short iterations, frequent working software, close customer collaboration, and rapid response to change. The plan still matters—but learning during development is expected to change the plan.

“Agile does not mean ‘no plan.’ It means plan a short horizon, build something useful, learn from reality, then plan the next horizon better.”

The Agile Delivery Engine

Core Agile Behavior

Five ideas make the process “agile”

⚡
Short Iterations

Sprints or iterations are deliberately short; a broad teaching range is about 2 weeks to 2 months, depending on the project and method.

📦
Frequent Working Software

Predetermined features are developed and delivered during an iteration, with working software appearing in weeks rather than months.

🤝
Customer Collaboration

Business people and developers cooperate closely so priorities and feedback influence development continuously.

🔄
Welcome Change

Changing circumstances are expected. Even late requirement changes can be incorporated into future iterations.

🎯
Value First

The team selects a manageable set of useful features rather than trying to implement everything at once.

One Iteration

What can happen inside a two-week sprint?

Day 1 · Sprint PlanningChoose high-priority stories, clarify acceptance criteria, split them into tasks, and agree on a realistic sprint goal.
Days 2–4 · Design + Start BuildMake just enough design decisions for the selected stories and begin implementation.
Days 5–8 · Build + IntegrateCode, test, integrate frequently, and keep the working increment healthy rather than postponing integration.
Day 9 · StabilizeFinish acceptance testing, correct important defects, refactor where necessary, and prepare the increment for review.
Day 10 · Review + AdaptDemonstrate the working software, collect stakeholder feedback, reflect on the sprint, and update priorities for the next iteration.

Build the University Registration System with Agile

Instead of attempting the complete registration platform in one long pass, the team delivers useful capabilities incrementally. Students and advisors can react to real software after every sprint.

Responding to Change

Agile changes the next plan—not history

Original Plan

Sprint 4 was expected to implement advisor approval and reporting.

→

Reality Changes

A new wait-list policy becomes urgent. The backlog is reprioritized: wait-listing moves into Sprint 4; reporting moves later. The team adapts without discarding completed increments.

Advantages & Disadvantages

Speed and flexibility come with trade-offs

✓ Advantages

✓Rapid, continuous delivery of useful software can improve customer satisfaction.
✓Working software is delivered frequently—weeks rather than months.
✓Business people and developers cooperate closely, often on a daily basis.
✓The project regularly adapts to changing circumstances.
✓Even late changes in requirements can be welcomed and reprioritized.

⚠ Disadvantages

!There may be insufficient emphasis on necessary design and documentation if the team interprets agility carelessly.
!New team members can find it harder to integrate when important knowledge exists mainly in team conversations.
!Total effort can be difficult to assess accurately at the beginning of the lifecycle.
!Frequent customer involvement is valuable, but it requires sustained stakeholder availability and decision-making.

Mentor Insight

Agile's strength is not simply that developers work faster. The deeper advantage is shortening the distance between a decision and evidence. Working software appears frequently, so assumptions are corrected while change is still manageable.

Key Takeaway

Agile process models use short sprints or iterations to develop and deliver predetermined features, repeatedly producing working software and adapting to feedback and changing circumstances. The model emphasizes rapid delivery, close business/developer cooperation, and welcoming change, while also requiring deliberate design/documentation discipline, effective onboarding, and realistic handling of uncertain early estimates.

Think Like a Software Engineer

A customer adds an urgent feature halfway through a sprint. Should the team immediately stop and implement it?

“Welcome change” does not mean “accept chaos.” Consider sprint stability, urgency, current work, product-owner priorities, and whether the change belongs in the current sprint or the next prioritized iteration.
