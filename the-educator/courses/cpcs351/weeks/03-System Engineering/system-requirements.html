System Requirements

Requirements begin before anyone writes “shall.” Engineers first understand the business, the current situation, the goals, and the gap between them—then translate that understanding into capabilities the system must deliver.

“A requirement is not a guess about a feature. It is a capability justified by a real need.”

Visual Hook

The Requirements Funnel

Information starts broad. Engineers collect facts about the business and its environment, identify needs, then narrow those needs into system requirements that can realistically be delivered.

BUSINESS CONTEXT Goals · environment · current operation · processes · users · problems
↓
COLLECT INFORMATION Presentations · observation · surveys · interviews · literature
↓
IDENTIFY BUSINESS NEEDS Find the discrepancy between current situation and desired goals
↓
DEFINE SYSTEM REQUIREMENTS Capabilities the system must deliver

FEASIBLE SCOPE A subset of needs selected under budget and schedule constraints
Requirements engineering narrows understanding into a deliverable scope. Not every desirable need can always be implemented in the current project.
Business Needs

A need is the gap between “now” and “goal”

Current Situation Bags are difficult to locate during transfers and passengers experience delays.
→
Business Need Improve baggage tracking, routing, and operational visibility.
→
Business Goal Move baggage accurately and efficiently while giving staff reliable status information.
ABHS Requirements

From business need to system capability

AIRPORT BAGGAGE HANDLING SYSTEM · SYSTEM REQUIREMENTS
R1
ABHS shall check in and transport luggage to departure gates and baggage claim areas according to the destinations of the passengers.
R2
ABHS shall allow airline agents to inquire about luggage status and to locate luggage.
R3
ABHS shall check all baggage and detect items that are prohibited.
R4
ABHS shall be able to serve 20,000 passengers per day.
Scope Reality

Needs are filtered by constraints

Business needs can be broader than what one project can deliver. Budget and schedule constraints determine which subset becomes part of the current system scope.

Information Collection

Five ways to understand the real problem

🎤
Customer Presentation Let the customer explain the business, goals, and expectations.
👀
Study Current Operation Observe how the current business actually works.
📝
User Survey Collect structured input from many users.
💬
User Interview Explore problems, priorities, and expectations in depth.
📚
Literature Survey Study existing knowledge, systems, standards, and prior solutions.
Focus of Information Collection

Nine questions that prevent shallow requirements

What is the business that the system will automate?
What is the system’s environment or context?
What are the business goals or product goals?
What is the current business situation, and how does it operate?
What are the existing business processes, and how do they relate to each other?
What are the problems with the current system?
Who are the users of the current system and the future system?
What do the customer and users want from the future system, and what are their business priorities?
What are the quality, performance, and security considerations?
Illustrated Story

The team that wrote requirements too early

Panel 1

Jump to features

The team immediately writes: “The system shall have a tracking dashboard.”

Panel 2

Ask why

Interviews reveal the real problem: agents cannot reliably locate luggage during transfers.

Panel 3

Define the need

The team identifies the business need: improve baggage visibility and location accuracy.

Panel 4

Write the capability

Now the requirement is grounded: airline agents must be able to inquire about baggage status and locate luggage.

Mentor Insight

Weak requirements often begin with premature solutions. Strong requirements begin by understanding the business, the current situation, the users, the environment, and the problem that must be solved.

Key Takeaway

System Requirements Definition begins by collecting information about business goals and the current situation, deriving business needs from the gap between them, and translating selected needs into capabilities the system must deliver. Budget and schedule constraints mean that only a subset of needs may be included in the current system.

Think Like a Software Engineer

A customer asks for “an AI dashboard.” Is that a system requirement yet?

What business problem, user need, operating context, quality concern, and measurable capability would you investigate before accepting that request?
