CPCS 351 / Topic 05 / Elicitation Process
Five-step learning cycle

Turn evidence into reviewed requirements.

Elicitation is iterative. Teams collect information, model the situation, derive requirements and constraints, test feasibility, and review the specification—then revisit earlier steps as understanding improves.

A model is not the goal. Use it only when it reduces ambiguity or reveals something the team could not see in prose alone.

The lecture sequence

Five steps, with feedback

1
Collect information

Study the business, users, goals, problems, policies, and environment.

2
Construct analysis models

Use informal or formal models when they help understanding.

3
Derive requirements

Formulate capabilities, constraints, priorities, and acceptance ideas.

4
Study feasibility

Check technology, time, cost, performance, and external constraints.

5
Review the specification

Find omissions, conflicts, ambiguity, and infeasible expectations.

Running example

University advising appointment system

Evidence

Students queue outside offices; advisors use separate calendars; peak-week demand is high.

Model

Actors: Student, Advisor, University Identity Service. Goals: view slots, book, cancel, record outcome.

Requirement

The system must prevent two students from booking the same advisor slot.

Desired properties

Requirements and models must survive inspection

  • Correct: represents the stakeholder’s intended need.
  • Consistent: does not contradict another requirement or policy.
  • Complete enough: covers important normal, alternative, and exceptional situations.
  • Feasible: can be delivered within technology, time, resource, and regulatory limits.
  • Verifiable: can be checked through review, demonstration, analysis, or testing.
  • Prioritized: communicates what matters most and when it is needed.
Acceptance thinking

Write the test idea while clarifying the requirement

Requirement: When two booking requests target the same available slot, the system shall confirm at most one booking and inform the other student that the slot is no longer available.
Acceptance idea: Submit two concurrent requests for one slot; verify one confirmation, one rejection, and one stored appointment.
Mentor insight

The diagram is circular because review can expose missing information, infeasible constraints, or misunderstood goals. Returning to stakeholders is progress—not failure.

Key takeaway

Requirements elicitation converts stakeholder and domain evidence into models, requirements, constraints, priorities, and acceptance ideas that are checked and reviewed.
