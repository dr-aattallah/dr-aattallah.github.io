CPCS 351 / Topic 05 / Collection & Analysis
Evidence before assumptions

Use multiple lenses to understand the problem.

No single elicitation technique reveals everything. Combine stakeholder conversation, observation, documents, surveys, stories, and models to triangulate the real needs and constraints.

Choose the technique according to the question. Interviews reveal meaning; observation reveals actual work; documents reveal formal rules.

Information collection toolkit

Six complementary techniques

Customer presentation

Hear business goals, strategic context, and the customer’s framing of the problem.

User interviews

Explore goals, exceptions, pain points, terminology, and priorities.

Observation

See actual work, handoffs, workarounds, timing, and tacit knowledge.

Literature survey

Study existing systems, domain research, product documentation, and benchmarks.

Forms, procedures & rules

Inspect business forms, operating procedures, regulations, and standards.

Surveys & user stories

Collect broad input and express value from a stakeholder perspective.

Question map

What the analyst is trying to learn

  • What is the business, its present situation, and how does it operate?
  • What is the system context and which external systems or policies affect it?
  • What processes, inputs, outputs, decisions, and handoffs exist today?
  • What problems, delays, risks, and workarounds occur?
  • Who are the users of the current and future systems?
  • What goals and priorities matter to customers and users?
  • What quality, performance, security, safety, and interface constraints apply?
Analysis models

Model only when it improves understanding

Intuitive models

Sketches, rich pictures, journey maps, affinity maps, and domain vocabulary.

Formal or semi-formal models

Use case diagrams, process models, state models, data models, and context diagrams.

Purpose

Expose missing information, conflicts, boundaries, dependencies, and constraints.

Value question

How much value or avoided cost does this requirement create?

Timing question

How soon is it needed, and what is the value of delivering it in this iteration rather than later?

Think like a software engineer

An official procedure says one thing, but users consistently work around it. Which is the requirement?

Investigate both. The procedure may be mandatory, outdated, or misunderstood; the workaround may reveal a missing business need or a compliance risk.

Key takeaway

Triangulate information from people, work, documents, and models. Then derive requirements from current problems, future goals, stakeholder needs, and external constraints.
