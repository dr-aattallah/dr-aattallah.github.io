Why elicitation is difficult

People speak in needs—not specifications.

Customers understand their work. Engineers understand software possibilities. Elicitation must bridge the gap without losing the meaning on either side.

Misunderstanding is not a “soft” problem. It produces wrong scope, costly rework, and systems that solve the wrong problem.

Communication barrier

The circular conversation

Analyst: “What do you want the system to do?”
Customer: “Tell me what software can do first.”
Analyst: “It can support many possibilities—but which problem matters?”
Customer: “I will recognize the right solution when I see it.”
Challenge map

Why real requirements remain hidden

Domain knowledge is uneven

The team may not understand the business; users may not understand software trade-offs.

Needs conflict

Different stakeholders can want different outcomes, priorities, or policies.

Quality stays implicit

Performance, security, safety, and usability are often assumed instead of stated.

Requirements change

Learning, regulation, competition, and new constraints reshape expectations.

Complexity is “wicked”

Understanding the problem changes the perceived solution—and vice versa.

Importance is underestimated

Teams may rush to design before confirming what success actually means.

Two essential categories

What the system does—and how well it must do it

Functional requirements

Information-processing capabilities, services, or behaviors the system must provide.

Example: The system must allow a student to search available course sections by course code, day, and start time.
⇄

Non-functional requirements

Performance, quality, safety, security, interface, platform, and other constraints on the solution.

Example: Search results must appear within 3 seconds under the normal registration workload.
Quality check

Make non-functional statements measurable

AreaWeak statementTestable improvement
PerformanceThe system should be fast.95% of searches complete within 3 seconds under 10,000 concurrent sessions.
SecurityThe website must be secure.Only authenticated students may submit registration requests; all traffic uses TLS.
UsabilityThe interface is user-friendly.A first-time student completes section search and selection without assistance in under 5 minutes.
InterfaceSupport mobile devices.Core registration tasks remain usable at viewport widths from 360 px upward.
Think like a software engineer

“The system must support many students.” Is this sufficient?

No. Ask how many, doing what, during which peak period, and with what response-time target.

Key takeaway

Functional requirements define system capabilities. Non-functional requirements define qualities and constraints. Both must be explicit enough to guide design and acceptance testing.
