CPCS 351 / Topic 05 / SRS & Feasibility
From discovery to agreement

Organize requirements so they can guide delivery.

A Software Requirements Specification records the agreed product context, functions, interfaces, constraints, qualities, and supporting information in a form that can be reviewed and maintained.

The lecture’s IEEE-style outline is a teaching structure—not a rule that every project must use the same document shape.

SRS blueprint

Four layers of a requirements specification

1. Introduction

Purpose, scope, definitions, references, and document organization.

2. General description

Product context, major functions, user characteristics, assumptions, and dependencies.

3. Specific requirements

Functional requirements, interfaces, performance, constraints, and quality attributes.

4. Appendices

Supporting models, domain details, traceability information, and reference material.

Feasibility study

“Valuable” does not automatically mean “doable”

Functional

Can the required behavior be implemented and integrated?

Technology & quality

Can available technology meet performance, security, reliability, and interface needs?

Time & cost

Can the work be delivered within schedule, budget, staffing, and operational limits?

External constraints

Are customer policies, standards, regulations, and government requirements satisfiable?

Important distinction

Requirements source versus current standards

Lecture baseline: the slides illustrate traditional IEEE 830-style SRS outlines. Preserve the organizational lesson: clearly separate context, functions, interfaces, performance, constraints, and quality requirements.
Engineering interpretation: modern teams may distribute this information across a living specification, backlog, models, acceptance criteria, and traceability tools—as long as the necessary meaning is explicit and controlled.
Mentor insight

A beautifully formatted SRS cannot rescue an infeasible requirement. Feasibility analysis should influence scope and priorities before the team commits to delivery.

Key takeaway

The SRS organizes requirements for agreement, review, planning, design, and testing. Feasibility checks whether those requirements can be achieved under real constraints.
