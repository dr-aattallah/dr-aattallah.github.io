THE LEARNING JOURNEY

Three activities. One engineering mindset.

Each activity targets a different stage of software engineering. Sets A, B, and C contain eight different challenges each, allowing different groups or sections to work on varied cases while practicing the same learning outcomes.

01

Requirements Detective

Discover what the client really needs.

3.33 marks
Identify stakeholdersWrite FR & NFRFind red flagsRepair requirements

Your team receives a client brief containing useful information and hidden requirements problems. Identify key actors, functional and quality requirements, expose vague or incomplete statements, rewrite them clearly, and determine what must still be clarified before development begins.

02

UML Design Challenge

Design the structure, then prove it can perform the behavior.

3.33 marks
Find 4–6 classesClass DiagramSequence DiagramDesign consistency

Translate a focused system scenario into a coherent structural design. Define candidate classes and responsibilities, create a compact UML class diagram, then model the target behavior with a sequence diagram using the same design objects. Revise the design when the behavior exposes a weakness.

03

Software Failure Investigation

The system failed. Find out why.

3.33 marks
Review incidentConnect evidenceFind root causeRepair & verify

Investigate a failed software system using an incident report and evidence board. Connect observed failures to engineering weaknesses, identify root causes, propose a concrete correction, and design a verification test that demonstrates the failure has been prevented.

IN-CLASS WORKFLOW

How Group Activities work

01 · RECEIVE

Your group receives one challenge from the assigned activity set.

02 · COLLABORATE

Work together for a maximum of 20 minutes and record one team solution.

03 · CHECK

Review your reasoning, consistency, and evidence before time ends.

04 · DEFEND

Any team member may be selected to explain and defend the group's engineering decisions.
