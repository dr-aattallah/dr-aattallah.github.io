Component 03 · Inquiry & Academic Communication

Research Component

An individual investigation in software engineering. Your goal is not to collect information; it is to frame a focused question, evaluate credible evidence, analyze what the evidence means, and communicate a technically accurate argument in a clear academic paper.

01

What this assignment develops

Research, critical analysis, technical accuracy and written communication.

Investigate

Understand a software engineering question deeply enough to explain its concepts, context, trade-offs and practical significance—not merely define terminology.

Analyze

Compare evidence, identify relationships and limitations, distinguish claims from support, and build conclusions that follow logically from what you found.

Communicate

Present a coherent technical argument using accurate software engineering language, logical organization, credible sources and IEEE citations.

02

Research workflow

A practical path from an assigned topic to a defensible paper.

STEP 1Decode the question

Identify the central concept, scope, comparison or relationship the question asks you to investigate.

STEP 2Build evidence

Search for credible academic and technical sources that directly support the question.

STEP 3Analyze

Group findings into themes, compare viewpoints, examine benefits, limitations and conditions.

STEP 4Write the argument

Organize evidence into a logical paper rather than a sequence of disconnected summaries.

STEP 5Validate

Check technical accuracy, citations, originality, clarity and whether the conclusion answers the research question.

03

Recommended paper architecture

The source assignment requires the provided formatting and structure guidelines; this expanded structure makes the expected academic flow explicit.

Cover Page

Course, research title, student information and submission details. The cover does not count toward the five-page minimum.

1 · Introduction

Establish the problem, define the scope, explain why the topic matters, and state the question or purpose of the paper.

2 · Background / Concepts

Explain the essential software engineering concepts and terminology needed to understand the investigation.

3 · Evidence & Analysis

Present the main findings using credible sources. Compare, interpret and connect evidence instead of simply listing it.

4 · Discussion

Explain implications, trade-offs, challenges, conditions, practical consequences and—where appropriate—contrasting perspectives.

5 · Conclusion & Recommendations

Answer the research question, synthesize the strongest findings and provide recommendations that are supported by the analysis.

References

List every cited source in IEEE reference style. The reference pages do not count toward the five-page minimum.

Minimum Length

At least five content pages, excluding the cover and references. Quality of reasoning and evidence matters more than padding the page count.

05

Evidence, citation & academic integrity

Credibility and traceability are part of the engineering argument.

Choose credible evidence

Prefer scholarly publications, recognized standards, authoritative technical documentation and reputable professional sources. Use evidence that actually addresses your research question.

Use IEEE documentation

Cite borrowed ideas and evidence in the text using IEEE-style numbered citations, and provide the corresponding full references in the reference list.

Write originally

Read, understand, synthesize and explain in your own academic voice. Proper acknowledgment is mandatory; plagiarism and unacknowledged copying are not acceptable.

07

Submission & final quality check

What must be true before you submit.

Submission requirements

  • Complete the work individually; collaboration is not allowed.
  • Submit a Word document through the designated course portal before the deadline.
  • Meet the minimum five-page requirement, excluding cover and references.
  • Follow the required formatting and paper structure.
  • Use IEEE citations and references.
  • Late work is subject to the course late-submission policy.

Researcher's final check

  • Does the introduction state a clear research question or purpose?
  • Does every major claim have credible evidence where evidence is needed?
  • Did you analyze rather than only summarize?
  • Are technical terms used accurately?
  • Can every citation be matched to the reference list?
  • Does the conclusion answer the question and follow from the analysis?
  • Is the writing original, clear and professionally presented?
