Class-diagram foundations

Class diagrams are UML’s central static-structure diagrams. They represent classes and their relationships and support object-oriented analysis and design. The source distinguishes a domain model class diagram, which represents things in the users’ work domain, from a design class diagram, which describes software classes.

Key relationship concepts

  • Generalization/specialization: organizes a superclass and more specialized subclasses; inheritance lets subclasses share superclass characteristics.
  • Aggregation: a whole–part relationship where parts can exist separately from the whole.
  • Composition: a stronger whole–part relationship where parts are treated as dependent on the whole.
  • Multiplicity: states how many instances may participate at each end of an association.

Graded/review work

Graded Work 1 · Certification training system
Model users requesting quizzes, system-selected questions, answer rating and hints, plus tutors who provide questions/hints and examiners who certify questions. Produce a use-case diagram and work out relevant use cases.
Graded Work 2 · Gifts Online
Analyze the online gift-purchasing scenario: authentication, host gift lists, search, viewing/selecting gifts, trolley, secure payment gateway, inventory/payment updates, confirmation email and delivery details.

Class-diagram exercises

University domain
Model university → faculties → departments; degrees administered by departments; students studying toward one degree; degree/course requirements; student enrollment multiplicities; and graduate/undergraduate specializations.
Directed graph editor
Construct two domain models for a digraph consisting of vertices, directed edges and labels. Show source/destination roles, names, labels, zero-or-more collections and the possibility that source and destination are the same vertex. Specify multiplicities on all associations.
