Lab 04 · Requirements Engineering

Functional & Non-Functional Requirements

Turn stakeholder needs into clear, testable statements. Distinguish what the system must do from qualities and constraints that govern how well it must operate.

What is a requirement?

A requirement describes either something the proposed system must do or a constraint on its development/operation. The complete set represents a negotiated agreement among stakeholders and forms the requirements document.

Functional requirements

Describe system behavior and services—what the system should do. A good functional requirement is specific enough to verify, such as requiring authentication with a user ID and password.

Non-functional requirements

Describe quality, performance, interfaces, constraints and other conditions on system operation. They are often harder to specify because qualities are relative and can conflict.

Writing discipline

  • Do not assume the reader knows what you mean.
  • Prefer precise verbs over vague terms such as “manage,” “handle,” “process” or “support.”
  • Write requirements rather than broad features: requirements should be measurable and testable.
  • Watch for conjunctions that hide multiple requirements.
  • Avoid unnecessary conditionals and uncertain wording such as may, might, probably or usually.

Lab activities

Activity 1 · Functional requirements
Select one scenario—mail-order warehouse, electronic voting, or multi-campground reservation—and write its functional requirements.
Activity 2 · Classify requirements
For an online book shop, classify statements about supported languages, book search, previous orders, 1000 transactions/hour, 24/7 availability, and removing books from the cart as functional or non-functional.
Activity 3 · Project transfer
Write the functional and non-functional requirements for your own group project.
