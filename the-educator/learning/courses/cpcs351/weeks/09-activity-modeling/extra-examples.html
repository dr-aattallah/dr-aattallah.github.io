Lesson 11 · Learning by Doing

Activity Diagram Practice Studio

You have already seen state lifecycles. Now deliberately change the question: model work, decisions, concurrency and responsibility rather than the condition of one object.

11of 13 pages
Learning destination

Translate realistic process descriptions into UML Activity Diagrams using actions, decisions, guards, fork/join, final outcomes and swimlanes—without duplicating the state-machine view.

Do not redraw the State Diagram. A state model asks “What condition is the subject in?” An activity model asks “What work happens next, who performs it, which path is chosen, and what can happen in parallel?”
1 · University Registration
Start with clean parallel checks + conditional approval.
2 · Food Delivery
Add operational concurrency + cancellation.
3 · Hospital Treatment
Finish with multi-role clinical branching.
Practice rule

Read the story and draw before revealing the image. Your layout may differ from the suggested solution, but the workflow semantics must be correct.

01
Activity A · Foundation

University Course Registration

A student submits a request to register for a course section. The system verifies prerequisites and available seats; these checks can run independently. If either fails, the request is rejected. If both succeed, the system checks whether departmental approval is required. Approval leads to registration; no approval requirement leads directly to registration; rejection leads to a student notification.

Decision + guardsFork / JoinStudentRegistration SystemDepartment
View in this order: (1) locate the Student/System handoff, (2) find the fork and join for prerequisite/capacity checks, (3) trace the approval decision, then (4) compare success and rejection outcomes.
Reveal Activity Diagram · University Registration

Activity lens: responsibilities and parallel validation. Do not interpret actions such as “Check prerequisites” as persistent states of the registration request.

02
Activity B · Intermediate

Online Food Delivery Order

A customer places an order. After validation and restaurant availability checks, restaurant preparation and driver assignment can proceed concurrently. Pickup requires the needed parallel work to complete. If a driver cannot be assigned or another required condition fails, the process follows a cancellation/refund path. Successful delivery ends with customer confirmation or rating.

Parallel operationsCancellation branchCustomerDelivery AppRestaurantDriver
View in this order: (1) follow validation, (2) identify the parallel restaurant/driver work, (3) locate the synchronization point before pickup, and (4) inspect the cancellation path separately.
Reveal Activity Diagram · Food Delivery

Activity lens: the diagram explains coordinated work. Earlier, the State Diagram explained the Order object's lifecycle; this diagram instead shows who does what and what can happen concurrently.

03
Activity C · Advanced

Hospital Patient Admission and Treatment

A patient arrives, registers and is assessed. Emergency and non-emergency cases follow different routes before examination. Required laboratory tests and imaging may run in parallel. After results are available, the clinical team decides whether the patient is discharged, treated/observed or admitted, with administrative bed processing when admission is required.

Emergency decisionParallel investigationsPatientReceptionClinical TeamAdministration
View in this order: (1) trace arrival and triage, (2) separate emergency from normal care, (3) inspect parallel investigations, (4) follow the final treatment/admission decision, then (5) check responsibility handoffs.
Reveal Activity Diagram · Hospital Treatment

Activity lens: focus on clinical/administrative workflow. The earlier Patient Encounter State Diagram focused on the encounter's changing condition, including composite investigation detail.

Reflection

Three activity diagrams, three new workflow ideas

Registration
parallel validation + optional approval
Food Delivery
parallel operational work + cancellation
Hospital
multi-role branching + parallel investigations

The domains may be familiar from state modeling, but the information shown is intentionally different. Reusing a domain helps you see the distinction between lifecycle and workflow without duplicating the model.
