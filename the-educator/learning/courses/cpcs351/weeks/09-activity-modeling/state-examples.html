State Machine Case Studies

Three domains, three levels of state-modeling complexity. Read each diagram as the lifecycle of one subject—not as a workflow of people and departments.

Reading rule

At every node ask: What is the subject now? On every arrow ask: Which event triggers the transition, and which guard (if any) permits it? A guard alone is not an event.

01
Basic lifecycle

Online Food Delivery Order

The subject is the Order. Follow its condition from creation through validation, restaurant acceptance, food preparation, driver assignment, pickup, transit and delivery. Rejected and Cancelled are alternative terminal outcomes.

StatesEvent-labeled transitionsAlternative terminal statesWaiting state
What to learn here: a state diagram is about the Order's condition. Food preparation and driver search may be activities elsewhere, but here they matter only when they change the Order's state.
02
Intermediate lifecycle

University Course Registration Request

The subject is one Registration Request object. This case adds validation, eligibility, guards, optional departmental approval, cancellation, rejection and successful registration.

GuardsConditional approvalComposite eligibilityMultiple final outcomes
  1. Submit: Draft → Submitted → Validating.
  2. Check eligibility: prerequisite and capacity checks determine whether the request qualifies.
  3. Approve or reject: eligible requests may bypass departmental approval or wait for it; invalid requests and failed approvals are rejected.
  4. Finish: a successful registration reaches Registered; cancellation and rejection end the request by alternative paths.
UML distinction: cancelRequest() is an event; [processingNotStarted] is a guard. Awaiting Approval is a state, not a transition condition.
Modeling assumption: the diagram uses explicit eligibilityPassed and eligibilityFailed events on transitions from the composite eligibility state. These are not automatic completion transitions. For a purely completion-driven model, use an appropriate final/exit-point design.
03
Advanced lifecycle

Hospital Patient Encounter

The subject is one Patient Encounter object. Emergency vs normal care changes the route, the illustrated investigations require both laboratory testing and medical imaging in concurrent regions, and treatment decisions lead to observation, admission or discharge.

Composite stateConcurrent substatesClinical branchingConverging outcomes
  1. Triage: the encounter follows either emergency stabilization or the regular doctor queue.
  2. Examine: the clinician decides whether investigations are required.
  3. Investigate: in this example, laboratory and imaging regions progress concurrently; both must finish before the composite state completes.
  4. Treat: the decision branches toward medication/observation, admission, or discharge preparation.
  5. Close: all successful paths converge on Discharged and the final state.
What to learn here: the dashed divider inside Investigations denotes orthogonal (concurrent) regions, not two sequential tasks. The unlabeled transition leaving the composite state is a completion transition.
Scope: this is a simplified successful-care teaching scenario. The chart does not model every clinical exception, reassessment loop, or adverse outcome; do not use it as a clinical protocol.

Do not memorize these domains. Learn the progression: identify a subject, name persistent states, label state-changing events, add guards only when needed, and introduce nested/concurrent detail only when it improves understanding.

Exam self-check

For each diagram, identify: (1) the modeled object, (2) an event, (3) a guard, (4) a final state, and (5) any composite or concurrent state. Explain why a state machine is different from an Activity Diagram.
