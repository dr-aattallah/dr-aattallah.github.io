Orders, Supply & Iteration

Use real business rules to combine decisions, repeated processing, synchronization, and responsibility.

10of 12 pages
Receiving an order

Line items + payment

  • Receive the order and process each line item.
  • If an item is in stock, assign it; if it needs reorder, trigger Reorder Item.
  • Authorize payment on a guarded path; failure may cancel the order.
  • Dispatch only when stock is assigned to all line items and payment is authorized.
Receiving supply

Incoming goods + outstanding orders

  • Choose outstanding order items that incoming supply can fill.
  • Assign goods to orders and repeat for each selected item.
  • If all outstanding items for an order are filled, the order may be dispatched.
  • Put remaining goods into stock.
Pattern

Iteration + guards + synchronization

Receive Order
→
each line item
→
[stock?] assign / reorder
→
[all ready + paid] Dispatch
Model the rule, not decoration

Every guard, loop, fork, and join should correspond to a real requirement. If you cannot explain its business meaning, reconsider it.
