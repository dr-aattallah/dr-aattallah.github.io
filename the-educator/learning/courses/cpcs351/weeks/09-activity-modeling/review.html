Integrated Review & Agile Guidance

Finish with model selection, construction checks, and the lightweight modeling discipline expected in software engineering practice.

13of 13 pages
State modeling checklist

Can you explain one object's lifetime?

  • Identify the subject object, states, events, transitions, and guards.
  • Distinguish persistent states from events and Boolean conditions.
  • Use nested states only when complexity justifies them.
  • Trace important transitions back to design operations.
Activity modeling checklist

Can you explain the workflow?

  • Identify actions, control flow, decisions/guards, and iteration.
  • Distinguish decision/merge from fork/join.
  • Use object flow when information or domain objects matter.
  • Use swimlanes to make responsibility and handoffs explicit.
Final comparison

When to use what?

Sequence
object collaboration and message order
State
lifetime and event-driven reactive behavior
Activity
business/system workflow and concurrency
Agile guidance

Do barely enough modeling

  • Work closely with customers and users.
  • Keep models high-level, lightweight, and visual.
  • Use collaborative modeling to resolve uncertainty.
  • Value working software over comprehensive documentation.
Self-test

Five questions before you finish

  1. What behavioral question does my diagram answer?
  2. Does every state/action have a clear domain meaning?
  3. Are guards precise and attached to the right alternatives?
  4. Did I confuse alternatives with concurrency?
  5. Can a teammate understand the model without my narration?
Topic 09 takeaway

Behavior modeling is not three disconnected UML diagrams. It is three complementary lenses: interaction, lifetime, and workflow.
