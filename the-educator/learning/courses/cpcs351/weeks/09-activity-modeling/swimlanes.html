Swimlanes & Responsibility

Make handoffs visible by partitioning workflow according to the actor, class, department, or subsystem responsible.

9of 13 pages
Core lecture

Responsibility partitions

  • Activity diagrams often involve several classes or organizational roles.
  • Swimlanes divide the diagram into responsibility zones.
  • Each zone represents a particular class, department, actor, or subsystem.
  • Cross-lane flows expose handoffs—often the points where delay, coupling, or ambiguity occurs.
Lecture-inspired example

Course registration

Student
Submit registration request
CourseSection
Check prerequisites
Verify capacity
Registrar/System
Check permission
Complete registration

Guarded “not ok” paths explain rejection; successful checks converge before registration completes.

Interpretation skill

Read the handoffs

Do not only trace arrows. Ask: Who owns this action? Where does responsibility move? Is information/object flow clear at the boundary?

Remember

Swimlanes add accountability to workflow. They answer “who does it?” while the activity flow answers “what happens next?”
