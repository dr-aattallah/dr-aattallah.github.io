Real-World UML Modeling: HungerStation & Snapchat

From a real-world system description to actors, use cases, candidate classes, and defensible UML models.

15of 15 pages
Worked examples Classroom discussion & self-checks
Learning outcomes

Analyze natural-language requirements, identify actors and functional goals, construct a use case diagram, distinguish classes from attributes, and justify UML class relationships and multiplicities.

Model review notes: UML actors are stick figures and use cases are ovals, not generic flowchart rectangles. Associations are solid lines; «include» points to the included use case; «extend» points to the base use case. In the class models, multiplicities have been checked, payment attempts are distinguished from a single payment, and Friendship explicitly connects two User roles. The diagrams remain simplified conceptual teaching models.

Scope and modeling integrity

These are educational models inspired by publicly visible app workflows, not representations of either company's proprietary implementation. Business rules are simplified and some details are explicit teaching assumptions. Use case diagrams capture actors' goals; domain class diagrams capture concepts and relationships. The detailed class diagrams below additionally show illustrative operations, making them closer to preliminary design class diagrams.

A repeatable modeling workflow

From Requirements to UML

01 · Read the scenario

Highlight nouns, verbs, constraints, and external parties.

02 · Identify actors

Find external roles interacting with the system boundary.

03 · Identify use cases

Express externally valuable goals using verb–noun names.

04 · Discover classes

Evaluate identity, responsibilities, state, and relationships.

05 · Validate models

Check multiplicities, constraints, and consistency with the scenario.

Do not confuse a use case with a program function. “Place Order” is a user goal; “calculate tax” may be an internal step. Likewise, a domain class is not necessarily an implementation class.
CASE STUDY 01

HungerStation — Food Delivery & Order Management

Primary challenge: connect customer ordering, restaurant processing, payment, and courier delivery.

Step 1 · Requirements

System scenario

HungerStation Food Ordering and Delivery System

HungerStation is a food delivery platform connecting customers, restaurants, delivery couriers, and payment services through a mobile application. Customers can create accounts, sign in, manage delivery addresses, and browse restaurants. They can search for meals, view menus, select products, customize available options, and add items to a shopping cart.

Before ordering, customers review selected items, adjust quantities, choose a delivery address, and view the estimated total including applicable delivery fees and discounts. To place an order, the customer selects an available payment method and confirms the purchase. The system processes electronic payments through an external payment service when required and records the order.

The restaurant receives the order, reviews its details, and accepts or rejects it. After accepting, restaurant staff prepare the items and update the preparation status. A courier is assigned to collect the order, travels to the delivery address, and marks the delivery complete. Customers can track order progress and receive status notifications.

After delivery, customers may rate their experience and contact support if a problem occurs. Restaurant staff manage menu availability, while platform administrators oversee participating accounts and operational issues.

Step 2 · Use Case Analysis

Identify external actors

ActorGoals / responsibilities
CustomerBrowse, manage cart, order, pay, track, rate
Restaurant StaffManage menu, review and process orders
Delivery CourierView assignments, confirm pickup, complete delivery
Payment GatewayAuthorize or process electronic payment
Platform AdministratorManage accounts and support issues
Boundary test: A shopping cart and an order database are not external actors. An external payment provider can be.
Step 3 · Functional Requirements

Extract use cases and relationships

ActorUse cases
CustomerBrowse Restaurants; Manage Cart; Place Order; Track Order; Rate Experience
Restaurant StaffManage Menu; Process Restaurant Order; Update Preparation Status
CourierView Assignment; Confirm Pickup; Complete Delivery
Payment GatewayProcess Online Payment
AdministratorManage Accounts; Handle Support Case

Relationship decisions

  • «include»: Place Order includes Review Cart and Select Address in this instructional checkout scope.
  • «extend»: Apply Discount extends Place Order when a valid promotion is available.
  • «extend»: Process Online Payment extends Place Order only when the online-payment option is selected in this teaching model. A separate Pay for Order use case can be preferable when payment has an independent user goal.
  • Association: Connect each actor to its externally visible goals.
Step 4 · Traceability

Walk through Ahmed's order

  1. Ahmed browses restaurants and selects two burgers.
  2. He reviews his cart and chooses a delivery address.
  3. He optionally applies a discount and confirms payment.
  4. The restaurant accepts and prepares the order.
  5. A courier picks up the order and completes delivery.
  6. Ahmed tracks progress and rates his experience.
Discussion: “Track Order” and “Prepare Food” concern the same Order but represent different actor goals. They should not be merged.
Step 5 · Domain Modeling

Discover candidate classes

CandidateDecisionWhy
CustomerClassIndependent identity and order history
RestaurantClassOwn identity, menu, and order responsibilities
MenuItemClassCatalog item with price and availability
OrderClassOwn lifecycle and status
OrderItemClassQuantity and purchased unit price belong to an order line
PaymentClassTransaction state and amount
AddressClass / value objectStructured destination details
PriceAttribute / valueA monetary amount, not necessarily an entity
OrderStatusEnumerationConstrained set of statuses

Why not store quantity on MenuItem?

One burger product can appear in thousands of orders with different quantities. OrderItem.quantity belongs to a particular order line. Similarly, OrderItem.unitPrice records the price at purchase time, while MenuItem.price may later change.

Step 6 · Structural Modeling

HungerStation class diagram

Important multiplicities

One Customer may place many Orders. An Order contains one or more OrderItems. Each OrderItem references one MenuItem. An Order may have a Delivery and may have several Payment attempts; payment status depends on the selected method and order lifecycle.

Modeling choice: Delivery

Separating Delivery from Order makes room for courier assignment, pickup, delivery status, and timestamps. A simpler system could initially model delivery information as order attributes.

CASE STUDY 02

Snapchat — Social Communication & Media Sharing

Primary challenge: model shared content, multiple recipients, conversations, friendships, and privacy.

Step 1 · Requirements

System scenario

Snapchat Social Communication System

Snapchat is a multimedia communication platform that allows users to exchange photos, videos, and messages, interact with friends, and publish temporary Stories. A user can create an account, sign in, manage a profile, search for other users, send friend requests, and manage friendships. Depending on relationships and privacy settings, users can communicate through private conversations.

Users capture photos or short videos using their device cameras and may edit the content with text, drawings, stickers, or visual effects. A user can send a Snap to one or more permitted recipients. The system delivers the content and records relevant delivery and viewing information. Content visibility and retention depend on the type of content and applicable settings.

Users can create Stories containing photos or videos and share them with an allowed audience. Authorized viewers can access available Stories, and owners can manage or delete their own content. The platform also supports chat, group communication, notifications, privacy settings, blocking, and reporting inappropriate content. Access controls restrict content to authorized audiences.

Step 2 · Use Case Analysis

Identify external actors

Actor / roleGoals
Snapchat UserManage profile, friends, Snaps, Stories, chats, privacy
Content RecipientReceive and view authorized Snaps
Story ViewerView Stories allowed by audience rules
ModeratorReview reports in the teaching model
Actor generalization: Content Recipient and Story Viewer can be specialized roles of Snapchat User. Only introduce separate actors when their distinct goals make the diagram clearer.
Step 3 · Functional Requirements

Extract use cases and relationships

CategoryUse cases
AccountManage Profile; Add Friend
CameraCapture Snap; Edit Snap
MessagingSend Snap; Select Recipients; View Snap; Send Chat Message
StoriesPublish Story; Select Audience; View Story
Privacy & safetyManage Privacy; Report Content; Review Report

Relationship decisions

  • «include»: Send Snap includes Select Recipients.
  • «include»: Publish Story includes Select Audience in this simplified workflow.
  • «extend»: Edit Snap extends Capture Snap at the optional post-capture editing extension point in this simplified model.
  • Independent goal: Report Content is not automatically an extension of View Story.
Step 4 · Traceability

Walk through Sara's Snap

  1. Sara opens the camera and captures a photo.
  2. She optionally adds a sticker and a short caption.
  3. She selects Ahmed and sends the Snap.
  4. Ahmed receives and opens the Snap.
  5. The system records Ahmed's viewing state.
  6. Sara publishes another Snap to a Story.
  7. Only users permitted by the Story's audience settings can view it.
Discussion: Updating viewing status may be an internal action within View Snap rather than a standalone use case.
Step 5 · Domain Modeling

Discover candidate classes

CandidateDecisionWhy

Why is SnapDelivery needed?

A single Snap can be sent to several recipients. Each recipient may view it at a different time. A single Snap.viewedAt attribute cannot represent those independent states. SnapDelivery records status and viewedAt per recipient.

Why is Friendship modeled separately?

When the relationship has its own data—request status, acceptance date, or settings—it can justify a Friendship class. A simple friend list may be enough for a much smaller scope.

Step 6 · Structural Modeling

Snapchat class diagram

Important multiplicities

One Snap can have multiple delivery records, each associated with one recipient. Each Friendship relates a requester and an addressee. A Conversation has two or more participants in this teaching model; real messaging platforms may support other forms.

Privacy is a requirement

Content access must respect audience permissions. Modeling a Story–Snap relationship does not by itself implement authorization; access rules must be specified separately.

Use Case Diagram

Question: What must actors accomplish using the system?

Focus: System boundary, actors, goals, and behavioral relationships.

Class Diagram

Question: Which concepts, attributes, responsibilities, and relationships support the requirements?

Focus: Structure, associations, multiplicities, and ownership.

Domain vs. design: A conceptual domain class diagram usually omits operations and technical details. A software design class diagram may introduce methods, interfaces, and implementation-oriented types.
Classroom assessment

Test your modeling decisions

Ask students to answer each question before expanding the explanation.

1. Is Restaurant a class or a property?

Class. It has independent identity, data, and responsibilities in this domain.

2. Where should the quantity of purchased burgers be stored?

OrderItem. Quantity varies by order, not by the catalog product.

3. Where should each Snap recipient's viewedAt value be stored?

SnapDelivery. Each recipient has an independent viewing timestamp.

4. Does every verb in a requirements paragraph become a use case?

No. A use case expresses an actor's meaningful goal. Internal implementation steps do not necessarily qualify.

5. Can Address be a class and also the type of a property?

Yes. A property of Customer or Order can reference an Address object.

6. Should every domain class have methods in the diagram?

No. A conceptual domain model commonly includes classes, attributes, and relationships without operations.

7. Is Payment Gateway an actor or a class?

External actor in a use case model when the gateway is outside the system boundary. An internal Payment class may still exist in the class diagram.

8. Can two different class diagrams model the same requirements correctly?

Yes. Multiple defensible models may exist, provided they satisfy the requirements and make their assumptions explicit.

Remember

Use cases explain what the system enables. Classes explain what the domain and design contain. Start from requirements, justify each modeling decision, and state assumptions explicitly.
