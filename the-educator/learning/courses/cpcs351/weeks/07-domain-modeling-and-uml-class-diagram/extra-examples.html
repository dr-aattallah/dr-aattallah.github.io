Extra Practice Examples

Practice the modeling decisions—not just the final notation—using small domains with deliberately different relationship types.

13of 14
Learning focus

Transfer the lecture rules to unfamiliar domains by identifying classes, attributes, relationship semantics, and multiplicities.

Practice 1

University enrollment

Story

Students enroll in course sections taught by instructors. Each section belongs to one course.

Candidate classes

Student, Course, Section, Instructor.

Relationships

Student enrolls in Section; Instructor teaches Section; Section belongs to Course.

Ask

Which multiplicities make the statements precise?

Self-check routine

For every practice domain, answer these six questions

  1. What concepts have independent existence?
  2. Which candidate nouns are only attributes?
  3. Where is there a genuine IS-A relationship?
  4. Where is there a whole–part relationship?
  5. What associations remain?
  6. What multiplicity makes each relationship precise?
Practice rule

A good domain model is not the diagram with the most boxes. It is the smallest model that captures the important domain concepts, their properties, and their relationships clearly enough for shared understanding.
