Inheritance, Generalization & Polymorphism

Use inheritance only when the domain truly contains an IS-A relationship—not merely because two concepts are related.

7of 14
Learning focus

Recognize generalization/specialization, apply the lecture’s IS-A and conformance tests, and connect inheritance to information hiding and polymorphism.

Inheritance

Generalization ↔ specialization

▲ Vehicle

Superclass: more general.

Car

Subclass: more specific. A Car is a Vehicle.

Boat

Another specialized form of Vehicle.

Features defined for the superclass are automatically defined for subclasses. UML uses a hollow triangle pointing toward the superclass.

Two lecture tests

Do not accept inheritance until it passes both tests

✅IS-A test

Every instance of the subclass must also be an instance of the superclass.

🔁Conformance test

Relationships of the superclass must also make sense for subclasses.

Counterexample

Why “Car is an Engine” fails

A Car contains or uses an Engine, but a Car is not an Engine. That is not inheritance. Likewise, the lecture’s Visiting Professor example shows that a proposed subclass can fail the conformance test when superclass relationships do not apply to it.

Related OO concepts

Inheritance, information hiding, polymorphism

🛡️Information hiding

Shield implementation detail to reduce change impact on other parts of a program.

🎭Polymorphism

One thing can assume different forms; the lecture illustrates this with overloading.

Remember

Inheritance means IS-A. If the relationship is “has-a,” “uses,” “works with,” or “contains,” another UML relationship is probably more appropriate.
