Topic 03 · Lesson 06

Architectural Design Diagrams

A diagram is useful only when its notation makes the engineering question easier to see.

6of 8 pages
Learning focus

Distinguish the architectural diagram types introduced in the lecture and select the right view for structure, interfaces, multidisciplinary blocks, or flows.

Four views · four questions

Choose the view by what you need to understand

Block diagram

Question: What are the major system parts and how are they connected? Best for a simple high-level structural overview.

UML component diagram

Question: Which software components depend on one another and what interfaces do they provide or require?

SysML BDD / IBD

Question: How do multidisciplinary physical and logical blocks relate internally and externally? Useful beyond software-only structure.

Data / material-flow diagram

Question: What moves through the system, where does it go, and what transforms or routes it?

Worked example

One ABHS, different views

A block diagram can show check-in, conveyor, scanner and routing subsystems. A UML component diagram can show interfaces among routing software, flight information and monitoring services. A SysML view can combine physical scanners, conveyors and software/control blocks. A material-flow view can trace baggage from check-in to aircraft loading. None is “the one correct diagram”; each answers a different question.

Misconception: “More detailed diagrams are always better.”

Extra detail can hide the question the view is meant to answer. Select the notation and abstraction level that communicates the required relationship clearly.

Key takeaway

No single architectural diagram shows everything well. Pick the view that exposes the relationship you need to reason about.
