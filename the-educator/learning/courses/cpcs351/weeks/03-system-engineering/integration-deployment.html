Integration, Deployment, and Maintenance

Subsystem success is not system success. The real test begins when independently developed parts meet one another and the operational environment.

7of 8 pages
Learning focus

Explain what integration, system testing, deployment and maintenance reveal that isolated subsystem testing cannot.

The integration reality

Interfaces are where assumptions collide

Format mismatch

Two subsystems encode or interpret information differently.

Timing mismatch

One component responds, samples or updates at a rate another cannot handle.

Physical mismatch

Devices, dimensions, power, connectivity or environmental assumptions conflict.

Operational mismatch

Human procedures or organizational workflow do not match the technical design.

What each stage proves

Integration assembles the parts and resolves interface incompatibilities. System testing evaluates end-to-end behavior against system requirements. Deployment introduces the system into its target environment, where real users, infrastructure and procedures matter. Maintenance changes the operational system while preserving compatibility across interdependent parts.

Worked example

New baggage scanner

The scanner firmware can pass every device-level test and still fail as part of the ABHS. Its messages must match routing software, its timing must match conveyor control, alarms must reach monitoring, staff must know exception procedures, and the deployment must coexist with the airport's current configuration.

Misconception: “If every subsystem passes its own tests, system testing is redundant.”

Subsystem tests cannot prove that interfaces, end-to-end flows, shared performance targets and operational procedures work together.

Key takeaway

The system is proven at the boundaries between parts and in the environment where the whole must operate.
