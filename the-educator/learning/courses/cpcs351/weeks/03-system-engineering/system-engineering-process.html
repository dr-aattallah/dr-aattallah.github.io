Lesson 02

System Engineering Process

System engineering moves from understanding the mission to defining requirements, designing the architecture, developing subsystems, integrating them, deploying the system, and maintaining the whole.

2of 8 pages
Core lecture content Added study material
Learning focus

Order the major system-engineering activities, explain the purpose of each, and trace one system need through decomposition and integration.

Process logic

Decompose first, integrate later

The process has two complementary movements. During definition and design, engineers move top-down from mission and system needs toward subsystems. During integration, they move bottom-up, combining developed subsystems and checking that the complete system satisfies its requirements.

Stage by stage

What happens across the process?

1 · System requirements definition

Study business goals, current situation, stakeholder needs, constraints and required system capabilities.

2 · Architectural design

Decompose the system into subsystems and define responsibilities and interfaces.

3 · Requirements allocation

Assign system requirements to hardware, software and human subsystems.

4 · Subsystem development

Different engineering teams develop their assigned subsystems under agreed interfaces and constraints.

5 · Integration & system testing

Assemble the subsystems and verify end-to-end behavior and interfaces.

6 · Deployment & maintenance

Introduce the system into its real environment and coordinate later changes across the whole.

Worked example

Trace one ABHS need

Need: move checked baggage to the correct aircraft reliably and on time. System requirement: defined throughput, routing accuracy and availability. Architecture: check-in, conveyor, scanner, routing-control and operator subsystems. Allocation: throughput and accuracy targets are distributed among them. Integration: baggage is tested end-to-end through the assembled system.

Misconception: “Once requirements are written, each discipline can work independently until the end.”

Subsystems are interdependent. Interfaces, shared constraints and allocation decisions require coordination throughout development; otherwise integration exposes expensive incompatibilities late.

Key takeaway

System engineering decomposes the whole into accountable parts, then integrates those parts back into a verified whole.
