System Requirements

System requirements define the capabilities and constraints of the complete system before responsibility is allocated to individual subsystems.

3of 8 pages
Learning Focus

Explain how business goals and the current situation lead to business needs, identify the information engineers must collect, and interpret the official ABHS system requirements.

From business gap to system requirement

Requirements begin with the business—not the solution

Business goals
→
Current situation
→
Discrepancies
→
Business needs
→
Feasible subset

System requirements

Business needs are derived from the gap between the desired goals and the current situation. Because budget and schedule are limited, the system normally satisfies only a selected feasible subset of all identified needs. The selected needs are then expressed as system capabilities and constraints.

Information collection techniques

Use multiple sources to understand the real system

Customer presentation
Study current business operation
User survey
User interview
Literature survey

No single technique gives the whole picture. Presentations reveal goals, observation reveals what actually happens, surveys provide breadth, interviews provide depth, and documents/literature reveal rules, standards and prior knowledge.

Focus of information collection

Nine questions engineers should be able to answer

1 · Business

What business will the system automate or support?

2 · Environment

What is the system context and what external systems, people and constraints surround it?

3 · Goals

What are the business goals or product goals?

4 · Current situation

How does the current business operate today?

5 · Existing processes

What processes exist and how do they relate to each other?

6 · Problems

What problems, bottlenecks, failures or inefficiencies exist in the current system?

7 · Users

Who uses the current system and who will use the future system?

8 · Wants & priorities

What do customers and users want, and which needs matter most?

9 · Quality, performance & security

What nonfunctional considerations must the future system satisfy?

Official ABHS system requirements

These are whole-system obligations

IDOfficial requirementWhat kind of obligation?
R1ABHS shall check in and transport luggage to departure gates and baggage claim areas according to passenger destinations.End-to-end functional capability.
R2ABHS shall allow airline agents to inquire about luggage status and to locate luggage.Information/query capability.
R3ABHS shall check all baggage and detect items that are prohibited.Security/screening capability.
R4ABHS shall be able to serve 20,000 passengers per day.Quantitative performance/capacity requirement.

Notice that none of these requirements says which software class, hardware device or human role implements it. That decision comes later during architecture and allocation.

Added clarification

Turn vague needs into testable statements

Vague need
“The baggage system should handle busy periods.”
Better requirement
State the measurable capacity, accuracy, availability or response target the complete system must satisfy.
Transfer example · Smart Hospital

From a hospital problem to a system requirement

Goal: reduce medication delivery delays and errors. Current situation: nurses frequently wait for pharmacy delivery and occasionally receive medication for the wrong ward. Need: faster, traceable, patient-safe medication delivery. A system requirement might state: “The system shall verify patient identity before medication release and record every delivery event.” A performance requirement could define a measurable delivery-time target. The exact robot, scanner or software module responsible is deliberately not chosen yet.

Apply it · Need, requirement, or allocation?

Statement: “Barcode scanner B shall read a medication label within 300 ms.”

Reveal reasoning: This is already a lower-level allocated/component requirement because it names a specific component and a measurable responsibility. A system requirement would state what the whole medication-delivery system must achieve without prematurely choosing the responsible part.

Misconception: “A system requirement should already identify the module or device that implements it.”

Not necessarily. System requirements describe what the whole system owes its stakeholders. Allocation later assigns that responsibility to subsystems and components.

Key takeaway

Understand the business and system context first; define whole-system capabilities and constraints second; allocate responsibility only afterward.
