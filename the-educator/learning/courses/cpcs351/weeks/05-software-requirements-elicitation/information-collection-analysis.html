Strong requirements come from multiple evidence sources, domain understanding and models that expose hidden assumptions.

Understand the domain

🏢 Business & context

How does the business operate? What surrounds the system?

🔄 Processes & problems

What flows, inputs, outputs, delays and workarounds exist?

👥 People & goals

Who are the users? What outcomes and priorities matter?

🛡 Quality & constraints

What performance, security, standards and policy limits apply?

Extended worked example · from story to specification

SkyGate Adventure Park — deriving requirements from a real operational story

Imagine a large fantasy-themed adventure park. Guests buy tickets online, arrive with families, enter through smart gates, reserve popular quests and rides, receive live waiting-time updates, purchase food and merchandise, and use a mobile app to navigate the park. Some attractions have age, height or health restrictions. Parents may link children to a family account. Operators temporarily close attractions because of weather or technical problems, while emergency staff must quickly locate affected zones and communicate instructions. Today, several activities are disconnected: ticketing uses one system, ride reservations another, restaurants use separate point-of-sale terminals, and staff sometimes announce closures manually. Guests complain about duplicate queues, missed reservations and discovering restrictions only after reaching an attraction. Management therefore proposes the SkyGate Experience System (SES): one integrated platform connecting guests, gates, attractions, staff and operational services.

🎟️1 · Plan

A family buys tickets and creates linked guest profiles before the visit.

🏰2 · Enter

Smart gates validate admission while preserving each guest's entitlements.

🐉3 · Adventure

Guests discover quests, check restrictions and reserve attraction slots.

📱4 · Adapt

The app reacts to queues, closures and schedule changes with useful alternatives.

🛡️5 · Operate safely

Staff monitor capacity, incidents and attraction status from one operational view.

FUNCTIONAL · what the system shall do

10 Functional Requirements

FR-01 — Account & profile: The SES shall allow a guest to create an account and maintain individual guest profiles, including linked child profiles managed by an authorized parent or guardian.
FR-02 — Ticket purchase: The SES shall allow guests to select admission products, dates and quantities, complete payment, and receive a unique digital admission credential for each eligible guest.
FR-03 — Gate admission: The SES shall validate a guest's digital credential at an entrance gate and record successful entry while rejecting invalid, expired or already-consumed admission credentials according to park policy.
FR-04 — Attraction discovery: The SES shall present available attractions with location, operating status, estimated waiting time and applicable age, height or health restrictions.
FR-05 — Eligibility checking: Before confirming a reservation, the SES shall compare the selected guest profile with the attraction's recorded eligibility rules and inform the guest when a restriction prevents participation.
FR-06 — Reservation: The SES shall allow an eligible guest to reserve an available attraction time slot and shall prevent the same guest from holding conflicting reservations.
FR-07 — Change notification: When an attraction is delayed, suspended or closed, the SES shall identify affected reservations and notify the corresponding guests of the change.
FR-08 — Alternative recommendation: When a reserved attraction becomes unavailable, the SES shall present currently available alternative attractions or time slots that are compatible with the guest's recorded eligibility.
FR-09 — Staff operations: The SES shall allow authorized operators to update attraction operating status, capacity restrictions, temporary closures and reopening information.
FR-10 — Incident support: The SES shall allow authorized safety staff to record an operational incident, identify the affected attraction or zone, and distribute an approved alert to relevant staff and affected guests.
NON-FUNCTIONAL · how well / under what constraint

10 Non-Functional Requirements

NFR-01 — Performance: The SES shall return attraction availability and estimated waiting-time information within 2 seconds for at least 95% of requests under the defined normal operating load.
NFR-02 — Gate performance: The admission-validation service shall return an accept/reject result to a connected smart gate within 1 second for at least 99% of validation requests under normal operating conditions.
NFR-03 — Availability: Guest admission, attraction status and reservation services shall provide at least 99.9% availability during published park operating hours, excluding approved maintenance windows.
NFR-04 — Scalability: The SES shall support at least 25,000 concurrently active guest sessions without violating the specified response-time requirements.
NFR-05 — Security: The SES shall require authenticated, role-based authorization for staff functions so that operational, safety and administrative actions are accessible only to approved roles.
NFR-06 — Privacy: The SES shall protect stored and transmitted personal data using organization-approved encryption and shall restrict access to child-profile information to authorized accounts and staff roles.
NFR-07 — Reliability: A confirmed reservation shall not be lost or duplicated following a recoverable service interruption; reservation transactions shall preserve consistency after recovery.
NFR-08 — Usability: A first-time guest shall be able to locate an attraction and view its current wait time and eligibility restrictions within three primary navigation actions from the app home screen.
NFR-09 — Accessibility: Guest-facing web and mobile interfaces shall conform to the accessibility standard adopted by the organization for production digital services.
NFR-10 — Auditability: The SES shall retain a timestamped audit record of staff changes to attraction status, capacity, restrictions and incident alerts, including the authenticated staff identifier associated with each change.
Study the distinction

FR asks “What observable service or behavior must the system provide?” NFR asks “How well must it work, or what quality/constraint must it satisfy?” Notice that both types are written as complete, testable statements—not one-word labels such as “security” or “performance.”

Remember

Evidence → understanding → model → requirement. The next lesson shows one important way to express high-level functional goals: use cases.
