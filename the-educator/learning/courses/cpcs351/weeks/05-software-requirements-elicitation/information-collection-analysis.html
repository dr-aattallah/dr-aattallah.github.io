Information Collection and Analysis

Good requirements come from triangulating evidence—people, documents, observation, regulations and models—not from one interview.

7of 9 pages
Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning focus

Select suitable information-collection techniques, investigate the business context, and use analysis models to derive requirements and constraints.

Official lecture techniques

Collect evidence from multiple sources

🗣 Customer presentation

Quickly establishes goals, terminology, scope and stakeholder expectations.

📚 Literature survey

Builds domain knowledge and reveals known practices, products and concepts.

📄 Business forms

Expose real data fields, approvals, exceptions and terminology already used.

📋 Operating procedures

Reveal current workflows, responsibilities and required controls.

⚖️ Regulations & standards

Identify mandatory constraints the future system cannot ignore.

📊 Stakeholder survey

Collect structured input from a larger population efficiently.

🎤 User interview

Explore motivations, pain points, exceptions, assumptions and tacit knowledge.

✍️ User stories

Capture a concise user-centered statement of role, need and value.

What should the analyst investigate?

Eight questions that reveal the system context

1 · Business

What is the business and how does it operate today?

2 · Environment

What external systems, organizations, policies or physical conditions surround it?

3 · Processes

What processes exist, what are their inputs/outputs, and how are they connected?

4 · Problems

Where do errors, delays, workarounds, duplicated work or unmet needs occur?

5 · Goals

What business or product outcomes should improve?

6 · Users

Who uses the current system and who will use the future system?

7 · Wants & priorities

What do stakeholders value, and which needs matter first?

8 · Quality concerns

What performance, quality and security expectations constrain the solution?

Constructing analysis models

Turn scattered evidence into a shared mental model

Formal or informal analysis models are not decoration. Their purpose is to help the team understand the application, requirements and constraints, expose missing assumptions, and give stakeholders something concrete to discuss.

From evidence to requirements

Analysis turns observations into engineering commitments

Current reality

Problems, goals, wishes, procedures, policies, standards.

→
Analysis & reconciliation

Resolve discrepancies, model the domain, clarify meaning and identify constraints.

→
SRS requirements

Agreed functional requirements, qualities, interfaces and constraints.

Worked example

University admissions

Interview applicants and admissions staff, inspect application forms and ministry regulations, observe the current review workflow, model the process, and reconcile conflicts. A manual “missing document” phone call may become a requirement for automated document-status notification, but only after confirming policy, data ownership and notification rules.

Remember

Do not ask only “what features do you want?” Understand the business system that creates the need, then derive requirements from evidence.

Technique selection

No single elicitation technique is sufficient

Interviews reveal reasoning, observation reveals actual behavior, documents reveal formal rules, and surveys reveal patterns across many users. Strong elicitation deliberately combines techniques because each source can expose blind spots in the others.
