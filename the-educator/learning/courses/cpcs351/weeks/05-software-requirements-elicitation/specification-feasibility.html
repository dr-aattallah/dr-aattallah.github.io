Requirements Specification & Feasibility

The SRS records what is agreed; feasibility asks whether the team can responsibly commit to it.

8of 9 pages
Learning focus

Recognize the major SRS sections and evaluate feasibility across technology, schedule, cost and external constraints.

SRS structure

Organize requirements so they can be reviewed and traced

1 · Introduction

Purpose, scope, definitions, references, overview.

2 · General Description

Context, functions, users, constraints, assumptions and dependencies.

3 · Specific Requirements

Functions, interfaces, performance, design constraints, quality attributes and other requirements.

4 · Appendices

Supporting material needed to interpret the specification.

Inside specific requirements

Know the major categories

Functional

Inputs, processing and outputs for required capabilities.

External interfaces

User, hardware, software and communication interfaces.

Performance

Workload, response time and throughput obligations.

Constraints & qualities

Standards, hardware limits, security, availability, maintainability and related attributes.

Feasibility

Desirable does not automatically mean doable

🧪 Technical

Can technology satisfy the functionality, performance and quality constraints?

⏱ Schedule

Can it be delivered in the required time?

💰 Cost & resources

Is it affordable with available people and infrastructure?

📜 External constraints

Do customer policy, industry standards or government rules permit it?

Worked example

Face-recognition check-in

The capability may be technically possible yet infeasible if privacy regulation blocks the proposed data use, required accuracy cannot be achieved, cameras exceed budget, or integration cannot be completed before launch.

Remember

Specification records the commitment; feasibility protects the commitment from being unrealistic. A requirement can be clear and testable yet still infeasible.
