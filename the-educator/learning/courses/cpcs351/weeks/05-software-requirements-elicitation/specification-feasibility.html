Requirements Specification and Feasibility

A good SRS says what is agreed; a feasibility study asks whether the team can responsibly commit to it.

8of 9 pages
Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning focus

Recognize the major sections of a requirements specification and evaluate feasibility across technology, time, cost and external constraints.

SRS structure

Organize requirements so they can be reviewed and traced

1 · Introduction

Purpose, scope, definitions, references, and document overview.

2 · General Description

Product context, product functions, user characteristics, constraints, assumptions and dependencies.

3 · Specific Requirements

Functional requirements, external interfaces, performance, design constraints, quality attributes and other operational/database requirements.

4 · Appendices

Supporting material needed to interpret or maintain the specification.

Specific requirements

What usually lives inside Section 3?

Functional requirements

Inputs, processing and outputs for each required capability.

External interfaces

User, hardware, software and communication interfaces.

Performance requirements

Workload, response time, throughput and other measurable performance obligations.

Design constraints

Standards compliance, hardware limitations, required technologies or mandated policies.

Quality attributes

Availability, security, maintainability and other desired system qualities.

Other requirements

Database, operations and domain-specific requirements that do not fit the categories above.

Feasibility

Desirable does not automatically mean doable

🧪 Technical

Is the required functionality, performance and quality achievable with suitable technology?

⏱ Schedule

Can the requirement be delivered within the required time window?

💰 Cost & resources

Is the required capability affordable with available people, infrastructure and budget?

📜 External constraints

Do customer policy, industry standards or government regulations permit the proposed solution?

Worked example

Face-recognition check-in

The capability may be technically possible but still infeasible for the project if privacy regulation prohibits the proposed data use, accuracy cannot meet the required threshold, cameras exceed budget, or the integration cannot be completed before launch. Feasibility is therefore a multi-constraint decision, not merely “can someone code it?”

Worked example

Real-time analytics dashboard

A stakeholder requests “instant” updates for millions of records. The engineer should turn “instant” into a measurable response-time target, evaluate architecture and infrastructure cost, and negotiate a feasible service level such as 95% of dashboard updates within 2 seconds.

Remember

Specification records the commitment; feasibility protects the commitment from being unrealistic.

Exam lens

Do not confuse requirement quality with feasibility

A requirement can be clearly written and testable yet infeasible because of cost, schedule, technology or regulation. Conversely, a technically feasible feature can still be a poor requirement if it does not reflect stakeholder need.
