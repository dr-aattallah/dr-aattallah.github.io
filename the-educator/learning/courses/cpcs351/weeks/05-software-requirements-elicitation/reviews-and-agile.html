Requirements Validation, Reviews & Agile Practice

Requirements become trustworthy when they survive technical, domain and customer scrutiny—and when detail is created at the right time.

9of 9 pages
Learning focus

Distinguish verification from validation, compare review perspectives and techniques, and explain agile requirements practice.

Verification vs. validation

Two different quality questions

🛠 Verification

Are we producing the requirements/models correctly? Check consistency, completeness, form and conformance.

🎯 Validation

Are these requirements for the right product? Check whether they reflect what customers and users actually need.

Three review perspectives

No single reviewer sees every defect

🧰 Technical review

Internal technical team examines engineering quality and defects.

🧠 Expert review

Domain experts check terminology, rules and assumptions.

🤝 Customer/User review

Customers and users check real goals, tasks and priorities.

Technical techniques

Peer review, walkthrough and inspection

👥 Peer Review

Peers examine requirements, often guided by a questionnaire.

🚶 Walkthrough

The analyst explains requirements while reviewers question assumptions.

🔎 Inspection

A checklist targets defects such as incompleteness, duplication, inconsistency and ambiguity.

Agile requirements practice

Enough detail for the current decision

🤝 Customer collaboration
🗺 High-level early requirements
⭐ Prioritize progress
✏️ Just-barely-enough modeling

Agile does not mean “no requirements.” It means avoiding premature detail that is likely to change and elaborating the next slice when it becomes relevant.

Topic 05 synthesis

The complete requirements story

  1. Discover the business problem and stakeholder needs.
  2. Separate capabilities from qualities and constraints.
  3. Collect evidence and model the domain.
  4. Express actor goals with use cases when appropriate.
  5. Derive and prioritize requirements, then check feasibility.
  6. Organize commitments in the SRS and review them from technical, domain and customer perspectives.
  7. Refine as understanding changes.
