Requirements Reviews and Agile Principles

Requirements become trustworthy when they survive technical, domain and customer scrutiny—and when detail is created at the right time.

9of 9 pages
Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning focus

Distinguish verification from validation, compare requirements review types, and explain how agile principles change the timing and amount of requirements detail.

Verification vs. validation

Two different quality questions

🛠 Verification

Are we producing the requirements and models correctly? Check consistency, completeness, form, traceability and conformance to the chosen process or notation.

🎯 Validation

Are these the requirements for the right product? Check whether they actually represent what customers and users need.

Three review perspectives

No single reviewer can see every defect

🧰 Technical review

Internal technical team examines the specification for engineering quality and defects.

🧠 Expert review

Domain experts check terminology, rules, assumptions and domain correctness.

🤝 Customer/User review

Customers and users check whether the requirements reflect real goals, tasks and priorities.

Technical review techniques

Peer review, walkthrough and inspection are not identical

👥 Peer Review

Peers examine the requirements, often guided by a review questionnaire.

🚶 Walkthrough

The analyst explains requirements step by step while reviewers question assumptions and raise doubts.

🔎 Inspection

A structured checklist targets common defects such as incompleteness, duplicated definitions, inconsistency and ambiguity.

Review aids

Use the right checklist for the right audience

The lecture recommends different review questionnaires and inspection checklists for technical, expert and customer reviews. A technical checklist may focus on consistency and testability; a domain checklist on business rules; a customer questionnaire on usefulness, priority and missing behavior.

Worked example

Library checkout requirement

“The system shall allow a patron to renew a borrowed item.” Technical review asks whether the requirement is unambiguous and testable. Domain expert review checks library rules such as holds and renewal limits. Patron review validates that renewal solves the actual user need and that required feedback is understandable.

Applying agile principles

Enough detail for the current decision—no more, no less

🤝 Customer collaboration
🗺 High-level early requirements
⭐ Prioritize and move forward
✏️ Just-barely-enough modeling

Agile does not mean “no requirements.” It means avoid prematurely specifying low-priority detail that is likely to change. Capture enough to make the current decision, then elaborate the next slice when it becomes relevant.

Topic 05 synthesis

Rebuild the requirements story

  1. Discover the real business problem and stakeholder needs.
  2. Separate required capabilities from qualities and constraints.
  3. Collect evidence from people, documents, observation and standards.
  4. Model the problem and express functional goals with use cases when appropriate.
  5. Derive and prioritize requirements, then check feasibility.
  6. Organize commitments in the SRS and review them from technical, domain and customer perspectives.
  7. Iterate as understanding changes—especially in agile development.
Final memory hook

Elicit → Model → Specify → Check → Learn → Refine. Requirements engineering is a disciplined conversation that continues as understanding grows.
