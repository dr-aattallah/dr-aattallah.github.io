Requirements Elicitation Process

Elicitation is not one interview. It is repeated evidence gathering, modeling, derivation, feasibility checking and review.

3of 9 pages
Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning focus

Apply the five major elicitation steps and explain how evidence becomes reviewed, prioritized software requirements.

Official lecture structure

The five major elicitation steps

1 · 📥 Collect

Collect information about the application, current business, users, environment, problems, goals and constraints.

2 · 🗺 Model

Construct formal or informal analysis models, when useful, to improve shared understanding.

3 · ✍️ Derive

Transform evidence, discrepancies, goals, needs, standards and policies into requirements and constraints.

4 · 🧪 Feasibility

Determine whether requirements are practical under technology, time, cost and external constraints.

5 · ✅ Review

Review the requirements specification for correctness, completeness, consistency and stakeholder agreement.

What information should be collected?

Ask about the whole operating context

🏢 Business & context

How does the business operate today? What external systems, policies and environments matter?

🔄 Existing processes

What are current processes, inputs, outputs and dependencies?

⚠️ Problems

Where are delays, errors, workarounds, duplicated effort or unmet needs?

🎯 Goals

What business or product outcomes should improve?

👥 Users

Who uses the current system and who will use the future one?

⭐ Priorities & qualities

What do users value most, and what performance, quality and security concerns exist?

Worked example

Clinic scheduling

Collect: interview receptionists and observe current booking. Model: map appointment flow and actors. Derive: formulate slot-search, booking and notification requirements. Feasibility: check integration with the hospital identity system and SMS provider. Review: walk through the resulting requirements with receptionists, doctors and technical staff.

Prioritization

Not every requirement has equal value or urgency

The lecture proposes discussing stakeholder willingness to pay and desired delivery timing as ways to expose priorities. The underlying principle is broader: force explicit trade-offs between value, urgency, cost and feasibility rather than treating every wish as equally mandatory.

Remember

Elicitation is iterative learning: evidence → model → requirement → feasibility → review → new evidence.

Exam lens

Given an elicitation activity, identify its purpose

An interview is a collection technique; a use-case or process diagram is an analysis model; a “shall” statement is a derived requirement; a technology/cost check is feasibility; a walkthrough or checklist is part of review.
