Derive, Specify & Trace Use Cases

Do not invent use cases from a blank diagram. Derive them from requirements, validate the actor goal, choose the right specification depth, then prove coverage.

Learning focus

Turn requirement statements into defensible use cases and connect every use case back to an agreed requirement.

The complete transformation

Requirement → candidate → valid use case → specification → traceability

📄Requirement

Start from agreed capabilities.

🔎Extract

Look for domain verb–noun tasks.

🚦Validate

Test the actor-centered business goal.

📝Specify

Choose the needed detail level.

🔗Trace

Prove requirements coverage.

1 · Finding candidates

Mine requirements for domain goals—not implementation actions

Look for domain-specific verb–noun phrases that mean “do something,” “something must be done,” or “perform some task.” Then identify the actor and the system/subsystem that owns the behavior.

2 · Validation gate

A candidate becomes a use case only if all four answers are YES

① Business process?

Is it a complete domain task rather than a click or algorithm?

② Begins with actor?

Can an external role initiate the business process?

③ Ends with actor?

Does the interaction return to the actor with an observable outcome?

④ Useful to actor?

Does it accomplish something valuable for that role?

All four must be YES.

“Query Database,” “Search Linked List,” and “Press Submit Button” may be implementation steps, but they are not complete actor goals.

3 · Use-case specification

One goal, three levels of abstraction

① Abstract

Reserve Attraction

A concise verb–noun phrase naming the business process.

② High Level

Start → goal achieved

TUCBW: the guest selects an available attraction slot.
TUCEW: the guest sees a confirmed reservation.

③ Expanded

Actor ↔ System steps

Describe the interaction step by step, including alternatives and exceptions when needed.

High-level rule: describe what begins the interaction and what useful result ends it. Do not fill the high-level use case with database updates or other background processing.

4 · Requirements–Use Case Traceability

Coverage must be visible, not assumed

A traceability matrix answers two directions: Does every functional requirement have use-case coverage? and Does every use case have a requirement/business justification?

Diagnostic power

A requirement row with no mapping suggests a missing use case. A use-case column with no requirement may indicate scope creep, an undocumented need, or an unnecessary use case. Priority/weight can also be carried into the matrix to guide development order.

Master rule

Requirements justify use cases; actors give them purpose; specifications give them detail; traceability proves coverage.
