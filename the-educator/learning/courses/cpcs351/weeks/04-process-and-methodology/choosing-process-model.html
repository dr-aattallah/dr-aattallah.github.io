Choosing the Right Model

Model selection is an engineering decision: choose the process logic that best manages the project’s dominant uncertainty, constraints and risks.

9of 11 pages
Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning focus

Evaluate project context and justify a suitable model using requirements stability, technology, complexity, reliability, schedule, cost, stakeholder visibility, skills, documentation and reuse.

Decision anchors

Start with the project—not your favorite model

🧊

Requirements

Stable and understood, or unclear and changing?

⚠️

Risk & Technology

Familiar technology, or major technical/safety uncertainty?

👥

Feedback

Do users need working software early to discover what they need?

📚

Control & Evidence

How much documentation, traceability, visibility and management control are required?

Context map

Five model instincts

🌊 WaterfallStable requirements · planning · documentation
🧪 PrototypeLearn requirements early
🌱 EvolutionaryGrow the real product
🌀 SpiralReduce high risk before commitment
🔄 AgileFrequent change · short feedback
Important: comparison tables are classroom heuristics, not scientific laws. Real projects often combine practices.
Decision lab

Choose — then justify

🏛️ Case A · Government payroll

Requirements are mature, interfaces are controlled, formal approvals and documentation are heavy, and change is limited.

Strong fit: plan-driven / Waterfall characteristics because stability and evidence dominate.

🧠 Case B · Experimental AI product

User needs are unclear and the team must learn what features are valuable while exploring unfamiliar technology.

Strong fit: Prototyping + Evolutionary characteristics because learning and product growth dominate.

🛡️ Case C · Safety-critical innovation

The project has high consequences, uncertain technology and expensive commitments.

Strong fit: Spiral / risk-driven logic because major uncertainty should be reduced before larger investment.
Memory hook

Choose the model that attacks the dominant uncertainty. Never answer with a model name only—justify it from project conditions.

Exam lens

Build an engineering argument

Good answer

“Spiral fits because unfamiliar technology creates high technical risk that should be reduced before major commitment.”

Weak answer

“Agile is best” or “Waterfall is old.” Popularity and age are not selection criteria.
