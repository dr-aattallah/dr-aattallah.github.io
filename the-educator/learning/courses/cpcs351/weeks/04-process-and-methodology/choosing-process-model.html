Choosing the Right Model

There is no universally best process model. The right choice is the one that best manages the project’s dominant uncertainty, constraints and risks.

9of 11 pages
Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning focus

Evaluate project context and justify a suitable process model using requirements stability, risk, feedback needs, compliance, team capability and other constraints.

Decision anchors

Start with the project—not with your favorite model

🧊
Requirements Stability

Are requirements well understood and unlikely to change, or still emerging?

⚠️
Technical Risk

Is unfamiliar technology, safety, performance or architecture uncertainty a dominant concern?

👥
Feedback Need

Do users need to see working software early before the team can understand what is really needed?

📚
Evidence & Control

Are formal documentation, traceability, compliance, visibility or management controls especially important?

🧭 Choose by Context
🌊
WaterfallStable requirements · strong planning
🧪
PrototypeNeed early learning · unclear requirements
🌱
EvolutionaryProduct can grow through real versions
🌀
SpiralHigh uncertainty · explicit risk reduction
🔄
AgileFrequent change · short feedback cycles
🧩 Complexity

Architecture size, integration burden and subsystem dependencies affect how much planning and risk control are needed.

💰 Cost & Schedule

Budget pressure, delivery deadlines and the cost of rework influence the acceptable process rhythm.

🧠 Team Capability

Skill, experience, coordination ability and management maturity influence which process can actually be executed well.

🔍 Visibility

Stakeholders may require frequent progress evidence or formal milestones.

♻️ Reuse

Existing components or platforms can change uncertainty, design effort and sequencing.

📄 Documentation

Some domains require heavier traceability and evidence than others.

Important: selection tables are classroom heuristics, not scientific laws. Real projects often combine practices rather than follow one pure textbook model.
Worked comparison

Same software discipline, different process logic

🩺 Medical-device software

High reliability, evidence, traceability and safety risk can justify stronger planning, documentation and risk controls.

🎟️ Event app

Fast-changing requirements and strong user-feedback needs can favor prototyping and short Agile iterations.

Memory hook

Choose the model that attacks the dominant uncertainty. Stability points one way; risk, learning needs and change may point another.

The section below is added clarification to make the concept easier to understand and revise.

Exam lens

Do not answer with a model name only

Good answer

Name the model, then justify it using specific project conditions such as stability, risk, feedback, compliance or schedule.

Weak answer

“Agile is best” or “Waterfall is old” are not engineering arguments. Model selection is contextual, not a popularity contest.
