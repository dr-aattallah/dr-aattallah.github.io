Spiral Model

Spiral development is iterative, but its defining logic is risk-driven commitment.

7of 11 pages
Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning focus

Explain how each spiral cycle identifies objectives, attacks major risks, engineers the next product level, and evaluates the result before further commitment.

Core lecture · Risk-driven loop

Each loop reduces uncertainty before commitment grows

🎯 Objectives & alternatives
🛡️ Risk analysis
⚙️ Engineering & verification
📋 Evaluate & plan next loop
Worked example

AI exam-proctoring system

Before full deployment, the team identifies false positives as a major risk, pilots detection on a limited population, measures error rates, adjusts thresholds, and only then commits to a broader implementation.

Memory hook · 🌀

Risk first, commitment second. Spiral asks: what is the biggest uncertainty, and what should we learn before investing more?

Added clarification

Connect it to engineering practice

Best fit

Large, complex or high-consequence projects where technical, cost, safety or requirements uncertainty makes early risk reduction valuable.

Exam lens

Keywords: risk-driven, uncertainty, alternatives, prototypes, iterations, risk reduction. Iteration alone does not make a process Spiral—the explicit risk logic does.
