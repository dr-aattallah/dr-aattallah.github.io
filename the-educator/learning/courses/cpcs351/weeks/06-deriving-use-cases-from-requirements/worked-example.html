Worked Example: From Requirements to a Use-Case Model

Follow one complete derivation from raw requirements to actors, validated goals, relationships, and a focused UML view.

9of 9 pages
Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning focus

Use UML use-case notation and style to depict high-level user requirements and write use-case-centric functional specifications.

Core lecture

What you need to understand

  • Start with requirement statements and identify the subject boundary.
  • Extract actors and candidate verb–noun goals.
  • Validate each candidate as a complete business task.
  • Add association, «include», «extend», or generalization only when their semantics are justified.
  • Keep the final diagram focused and readable.

Worked example

Airport Check-In and Security Screening

Passenger is the general actor; Tour Guide, Minor Passenger, and Passenger with Special Needs specialize the role. Group Check-In can reuse Individual Check-In; baggage-related behavior is optional for passengers without luggage.

Remember

A strong use-case model is a compact explanation of actor goals, scope, reuse, optional behavior, and specialization—not a map of every internal step.
