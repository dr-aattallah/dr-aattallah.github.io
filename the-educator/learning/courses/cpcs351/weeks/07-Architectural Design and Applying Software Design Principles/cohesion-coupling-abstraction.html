Cohesion, Coupling, KISS & Abstraction

Keep related responsibilities together, dependencies low, designs simple, and details hidden behind useful abstractions.

13of 14
  • High cohesion: keep related things together and unrelated things out; this improves understandability and changeability.
  • Cohesion measures how relevant a module’s functions are to its core functionality and facilitates understanding, reuse, and maintenance.
  • Types listed in the source: functional, layer, communicational, sequential, procedural, temporal, and utility cohesion.
  • Low coupling: reduce interdependencies between modules so changes and runtime effects do not propagate unpredictably.
  • Types listed: content, common, control, stamp, data, routine call, type use, inclusion/import, and external coupling.
  • High coupling increases uncertainty of runtime effects and change impact; low coupling supports understanding, testing, reuse, and maintenance.
  • KISS: favor simple, straightforward, easy-to-understand designs; in OO design the source describes deliberately simple “stupid objects.”
  • High abstraction: hide or defer unnecessary details to reduce complexity.
  • A good abstraction provides information hiding and lets you understand the essence of a subsystem without unnecessary implementation detail.
