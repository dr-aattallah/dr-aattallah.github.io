The Nature of Software

Software is intangible, cheap to reproduce, labor-intensive to develop, easy to change, and vulnerable to structural deterioration through repeated change.

2of 11 pages
Before You StartA car physically wears out. Software does not. So why does old software often become harder and more expensive to change?

The answer is central to why software needs engineering discipline.

Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning Focus

Explain at least four distinctive properties of software and use each property to predict a practical engineering consequence.

Core Lecture

Five Properties That Change the Engineering Problem

  • Intangible: effort and progress are harder to see directly.
  • Cheap to reproduce: most cost is in development and evolution rather than manufacturing copies.
  • Labor-intensive: development is difficult to automate completely.
  • Easy to modify: changes are technically possible, but unsafe changes can damage structure.
  • Does not physically wear out: deterioration comes mainly from accumulated change, dependencies, and complexity.
Visual Model

Physical Product vs. Software Product

Physical product
Manufacturing and material wear matter greatly.
Software
Development and evolution dominate; change creates structural risk.
Worked Example

Campus Registration Rule Change

A policy change that sounds like “one line” can affect validation logic, database constraints, user messages, tests, reports, documentation, and integrations. The copy cost is almost zero; the understanding and change cost is not.

Remember

Software is easy to copy and technically easy to edit, but difficult to understand, coordinate, and evolve safely.

Common Misconception

“Because software is easy to modify, software changes are cheap.” Editing text is easy; understanding dependencies, preserving quality, retesting behavior, and coordinating releases can be expensive.

Scenario
