Topic 01 · Lesson 01

The Nature of Software

Before asking how to engineer software, understand why software behaves differently from physical products.

1of 8 lessons
Start with the puzzleIf software has no factory, no raw material, and does not physically wear out, where do its cost and difficulty come from?
Learning Focus

Explain at least four distinctive properties of software and connect each property to an engineering consequence.

Visual Anchor · The Software Iceberg
What users see

Screens, features, responses and releases.

What engineering must manage

Requirements, design decisions, dependencies, quality, coordination, change, testing and evolution.

Must Know

Why Software Is Different

  • Software is intangible. Because it cannot be physically inspected like a bridge or machine, development effort and progress are harder to understand.
  • Software is easy to reproduce. Once developed, copying it is cheap; most cost lies in development rather than manufacturing.
  • The industry is labor-intensive. Much of the intellectual work is difficult to automate completely.
  • Untrained people can hack something together. A program may appear to work while serious quality problems remain hidden.
  • Software is easy to modify. This encourages changes, sometimes without understanding their wider consequences.
  • Software does not physically “wear out.” It deteriorates when its design is changed incorrectly or in unanticipated ways, increasing complexity.
Official Idea, Explained

Copying Is Cheap; Creating Is Not

Manufacturing another physical product usually consumes material and factory effort. For software, making another copy is nearly free. The expensive part is discovering what to build, designing it, implementing it, checking it, coordinating people, and changing it safely.

Why “easy to modify” can become dangerous

Ease of editing does not mean ease of understanding. A small local change can violate assumptions elsewhere and gradually make the design harder to maintain.

Quick Compare

A physical component fails because it wore down after years of use. A software module becomes unreliable after years of poorly controlled changes. Are these the same kind of deterioration?

Self-feedback: No. The software did not physically wear out; its structure and assumptions were degraded by change. That distinction is central to the chapter.
Remember

Software's unusual economics and changeability are exactly why disciplined engineering is needed.
