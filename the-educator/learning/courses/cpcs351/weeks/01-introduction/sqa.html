Software Quality Assurance

SQA builds evidence that both the engineering process and the produced artifacts meet expected requirements and quality standards.

9of 11 pages
Before You StartIf testing happens only after coding is finished, when do you discover that the requirements themselves were ambiguous?

SQA begins much earlier than final testing.

Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning Focus

Distinguish SQA from testing and explain, with examples, how SQA evaluates process quality and artifact quality throughout the software life cycle.

Core Lecture

Quality Assurance Is Broader Than Testing

  • SQA evaluates both process quality and product/artifact quality.
  • It operates throughout the life cycle, not only at final testing.
  • Reviews, verification, validation, testing, standards, and measurements support quality assurance.
Visual Model

Two Sides of SQA

Process Quality
Are activities performed properly and consistently?
Artifact Quality
Do requirements, designs, code, and releases meet expectations?
Worked Example

Medication Ordering System

A requirements review can catch ambiguous dosage rules before coding; a design review can expose unsafe responsibility assignments; testing later checks implemented behavior. All three contribute to quality assurance at different points.

Remember

Testing is one quality activity; SQA is the broader continuous effort to assure the process and its outputs.

Common Misconception

“SQA is the testing team at the end.” That view is too narrow. Quality problems can originate in requirements, design, process, or implementation, so assurance must begin before code exists.

Added Practice

Catch the Defect Earlier

Problem

A requirement says the system should respond “quickly.” What SQA activity could detect the problem before implementation?

Self-Feedback

A requirements review can challenge the ambiguity and ask for a measurable response-time criterion. Catching it early is cheaper and clearer than discovering it during acceptance testing.
