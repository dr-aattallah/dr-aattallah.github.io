Waterfall Process

Waterfall organizes major development work into clearly staged activities connected by explicit deliverables.

8of 11 pages
Before You StartIf requirements change every week, what happens to a process that depends on committing strongly to earlier-stage deliverables?

Use that tension to understand both the appeal and the limitation of Waterfall.

Core lecture content Clarification & study aids
Learning Focus

Describe the major Waterfall stages, explain the role of deliverables between stages, and evaluate whether the model fits a short project scenario.

Core Lecture

Staged Development With Explicit Deliverables

  • Major stages include system engineering, requirements analysis, design, coding and unit testing, integration testing, acceptance testing, and maintenance.
  • Each stage produces outputs that feed the next stage.
  • The model provides a clear life-cycle reference when requirements are relatively stable and coordination/documentation are important.
  • Its main tension is accommodating significant change after earlier commitments have been made.

Worked Example

Payroll Replacement Project

When regulations, interfaces, and acceptance criteria are stable, the team can baseline requirements, design against them, implement, verify, and release in planned stages with clear review points.

Remember

Waterfall gains clarity from staged commitment; that same commitment makes late change more expensive to absorb.

Common Misconception

“Waterfall means nobody ever revisits an earlier decision.” The key idea is staged progression with stronger handoffs and commitments. Real projects may still correct earlier work, but change is less fluid than in highly iterative approaches.

Added Practice

Would Waterfall Fit?

Case A
Stable payroll rules, fixed interfaces, formal approval gates.
Case B
New consumer app with uncertain features and weekly user feedback.

A strong answer argues that Case A is more compatible with Waterfall's staged commitments, while Case B creates more pressure for repeated learning and change.
