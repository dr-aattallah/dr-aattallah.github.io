The Nature of Software

Before asking how to engineer software, understand why software behaves differently from physical products.

If software has no factory, no raw material, and does not physically wear out, where do its cost and difficulty come from?
Learning Focus

The chapter is one causal story

Nature of Software→Need for Engineering→Teamwork & Scale→Life-Cycle Activities→SQA + PM→OOSE

Software's unusual nature creates engineering difficulty; scale requires coordinated teams; teams need a life-cycle process; development is supported by quality assurance and project management; OOSE specializes engineering around interacting objects.

Visual Anchor

The Software Iceberg

What users seeScreens, features, responses and releases.
What engineering must manageRequirements, design decisions, dependencies, quality, coordination, change, testing and evolution.
Engineering idea: the visible product is only part of the work. Much of the cost and difficulty lives below the surface.
Must Know

Why Software Is Different · 1 of 2

IntangibleIt cannot be physically inspected like a bridge or machine, so development effort and progress are harder to understand.
Easy to reproduceOnce developed, copying is cheap; most cost lies in development rather than manufacturing.
Labor-intensiveMuch of the intellectual work is difficult to automate completely.
Must Know

Why Software Is Different · 2 of 2

Untrained people can hack something togetherA program may appear to work while serious quality problems remain hidden.
Easy to modifyThis encourages changes, sometimes without understanding their wider consequences.
Does not physically wear outIt deteriorates when design is changed incorrectly or in unanticipated ways, increasing complexity.
Official Idea, Explained

Copying Is Cheap; Creating Is Not

DiscoverWhat should be built?
DesignHow should it work?
Build & CheckImplement and validate it.
Coordinate & ChangeEvolve it safely over time.
Manufacturing another physical product consumes material and factory effort. For software, another copy is nearly free. The expensive part is discovering, designing, implementing, checking, coordinating and changing safely.
Supporting Detail

Why “easy to modify” can become dangerous

Ease of editing does not mean ease of understanding.

A small local change can violate assumptions elsewhere and gradually make the design harder to maintain.
Apply It · Banking App

A “small” rule can create a system-wide change

International transfers above a threshold now require an additional approval. Why can this require requirements analysis, design review, coding, testing, security review and coordinated release?
Reasoning: the rule can affect UI, business logic, authorization, audit records, tests and operational procedures. Cheap code editing does not mean cheap or safe system change.
Remember

The Nature of Software

Software's unusual economics and changeability are exactly why disciplined engineering is needed.

What Is Software Engineering?

Software engineering applies engineering processes and methods to improve the way software is produced and the quality of what is delivered.

If coding faster reduces time but damages quality, did the project really improve?
Official Definition

Software Engineering as a Discipline

Software engineering focuses on the research, education, and application of engineering processes and methods to significantly increase software productivity and quality while reducing software cost and time to market.

Visual Anchor · PQCT Dashboard

Four pressures must be balanced

P ↑Productivity
Q ↑Quality
C ↓Cost
T ↓Time to Market

The goal is not to optimize one number blindly. Engineering decisions balance all four pressures.

Worked Example

A Release Trade-off

ShortcutA team shortens time to market by skipping reviews and tests.
ConsequenceQuality may fall and later cost may increase.
Software engineering seeks a repeatable process that improves the overall PQCT outcome rather than one metric in isolation.
Make the Trade-off Visible

“We wrote twice as many lines of code.”

A manager calls this improved productivity. What is missing from the claim?
Self-feedback: Lines of code alone say little about delivered value. Ask what happened to quality, cost, time to market, and useful functionality.

Topic 01 · Lesson 03

Why Software Engineering?

As software spreads, grows, and becomes expensive and critical, “just code it” stops being a viable development strategy.

Could one engineer build a 500,000-line system alone in a reasonable time?
Must Know

Why an Engineering Approach Is Needed

  • Software is expanding into all sectors of society; companies rely on it to run and expand their businesses.
  • Systems are becoming larger and more complex, reaching millions of lines of code.
  • The chapter notes that software can represent 90–95% of total system costs, compared with 5–10% two decades earlier.
  • Embedded systems may contain costly ASICs, making software quality critical.
Visual Anchor · The Scale Argument

Scale makes teamwork unavoidable

10,000 LOC100–200 engineer-days
≈ 5–10 months for one engineer
→
500,000 LOC5,000–10,000 engineer-days
≈ 20–40 years for one engineer
Conclusion: real-world systems require many software engineers working together.
Official Evidence

50–100 Lines per Day Is Not “Slow Typing”

The chapter uses an average of 50–100 source lines per day to make a larger point.

Understand & DesignReason before coding.
Coordinate & IntegrateWork with people and components.
Test & CorrectProduce reliable evidence.
Estimate Before You Memorize

Why can't better typing tools solve scale?

If a system is fifty times larger, why can't one engineer simply finish it fifty times faster?
Self-feedback: Scale adds intellectual complexity and forces teamwork. Teamwork then introduces conceptualization, communication and coordination problems.

Engineering Software as a Team

Large systems force people to work together. The next engineering problem is making sure they understand, communicate, and coordinate around the same system.

Four engineers can all be correct about their own piece—and still build the wrong whole.
Visual Anchor

Why Software Engineering?

Official Lecture Structure

Three Teamwork Challenges

CommunicationExpress ideas precisely enough that others interpret them consistently.
CoordinationAlign interdependent work so components, people and decisions fit together.
The Engineering Response

How software engineering supports teams

Processes & MethodologiesSupport analysis and design.
UMLProvides a shared visual language for communication and coordination.

Interpret the Elephant

Different views can all be partial

A database engineer says “the system is the data model,” while a UI engineer says “the system is the user journey.” What is the problem?
Self-feedback: Neither view is necessarily false; each is partial. The conceptualization challenge is to create a shared model of the whole, then communicate and coordinate decisions across views.

Software Life Cycle — The Big Picture

Software engineering is not one chain of coding steps. The chapter organizes it as three interacting life-cycle tracks.

While developers design and code, who protects quality—and who keeps the project feasible, scheduled and controlled?
Visual Anchor

Software Life Cycle Activities

One integrated view of the three life-cycle tracks and their shared PQCT goals.

Read the Visual

Three Interacting Tracks

Software Development ProcessTransforms the initial concept into an operational system.
Software Quality AssuranceChecks that activities are performed properly and artifacts meet requirements and quality standards.
Software Project ManagementControls and administers development and SQA work.
Important: these are interacting tracks—not Development → then SQA → then Project Management.
Connect the Tracks to PQCT

Each track contributes differently

DevelopmentCreates the product.
SQAProvides confidence in process and artifacts.
Project ManagementControls effort, schedule, risk and administration.

Together they aim to improve Productivity and Quality while controlling Cost and Time to Market.

Map the Responsibility

Which track primarily owns each activity?

Designing an architecture · reviewing a requirements document · estimating effort and scheduling a release.
Self-feedback: Development → architecture. SQA → review for conformance/quality. Project Management → estimation and scheduling.

Development Process & Waterfall

Follow development from the initial system concept to an operational system—and connect every phase to the artifact or evidence it produces.

What has to happen between “we need a system” and “the system is running in its target environment”?
Development Process

What the Process Must Do

A software development process transforms the initial system concept into the operational system running in the target environment.

UnderstandIdentify business needs and conduct a feasibility study.
TransformFormulate requirements/capabilities, then design, implement, test and deploy.
Visual Anchor · Waterfall Cascade

The Seven Phases

1 · System Engineering
2 · Requirements Analysis
3 · Software Design
4 · Coding & Unit Testing
5 · Integration & Integration Testing
6 · Acceptance Testing
7 · Maintenance
Phase → Evidence · 1 of 2

What Each Phase Produces

1 · System EngineeringSystem concept, business need, scope/context and feasibility evidence.
2 · Requirements AnalysisSRS or equivalent agreed requirements.
3 · Software DesignArchitecture and detailed design models/specifications.
Phase → Evidence · 2 of 2

What Each Phase Produces

4 · Coding & Unit TestingSource code, executable units and unit-test results.
5 · Integration & TestingIntegrated build, integration-test results and defect evidence.
6 · Acceptance TestingEvidence that the delivered system satisfies agreed needs.
7 · MaintenanceChange requests, fixes, revised artifacts and updated releases.

Exact document names vary by organization, but each phase should leave evidence that supports the next.

Worked Example · University Registration System

From Need to Integrated System

NeedStudents need online registration.
SRSRules, courses, prerequisites and capacity.
DesignUI, services, database and interfaces.
CodeRegistration modules + unit tests.
IntegrateConnect SIS, authentication and billing.
AcceptRegistrar/students validate scenarios.
Worked Example · Continued

Maintenance keeps the system useful

MaintainFix defects and adapt policies after delivery.

This example applies the lecture’s phase sequence to one coherent system so students can see how information and artifacts flow forward.

How to Read It

Waterfall Organizes Development Work

Understand context and requirements before design; design before implementation; test units and integrations; validate through acceptance testing; then maintain the delivered system.

Later topics can critique or compare lifecycle models. Here, the priority is understanding the sequence and purpose of these phases.
Put the Phases Back in Order

What evidence flows forward?

Design · Acceptance Testing · Requirements Analysis · Coding & Unit Testing. Which comes first?
Self-feedback: Requirements Analysis → Design → Coding & Unit Testing → Acceptance Testing. Requirements guide design; design guides implementation; implementation produces executable software; acceptance provides evidence against agreed needs.

Topic 01 · Lesson 07

SQA & Project Management

Development builds the system. SQA asks whether the work and artifacts meet expectations; project management asks whether the work is controlled and deliverable.

A project can produce high-quality code and still miss the deadline. It can also finish on time and deliver poor quality. Who watches each problem?
Visual Anchor

Quality Gate vs Control Tower

Quality Gate · SQAEnsures development activities are performed properly and software artifacts meet requirements and desired quality standards.
Control Tower · Project ManagementOversees control and administration of development and SQA activities so delivery remains manageable.
Project Management Activities

Responsibilities Listed in the Chapter

Effort EstimationUnderstand expected work.
Planning & SchedulingOrganize work over time.
Risk ManagementIdentify and control uncertainty.
Project AdministrationControl and coordinate delivery on time and within budget.
Spot the Responsibility

SQA or Project Management?

“The requirements review found an ambiguous rule.” · “The release is two weeks behind schedule.”
Self-feedback: The review finding is primarily SQA; schedule control is primarily project management. The tracks interact, but their purposes are different.
Remember

Three tracks, three purposes

SQA protects process/artifact quality; project management controls the project. Neither replaces development.
