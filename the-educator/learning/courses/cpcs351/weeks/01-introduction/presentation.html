Why software behaves differently from physical products — and why that changes how we engineer it.

The Educator · Topic 01 · Lesson 01
Open by asking students what makes software different from a car, bridge, or phone. Do not answer immediately.
Start with the puzzle

Where do software's cost and difficulty come from?

If software has no factory, no raw material, and does not physically wear out, why can building and changing it still be expensive and difficult?

Think first. We will return to this question.

Pause here. Ask for two or three student answers before moving forward.

Must Know

Why software is different

IntangibleHarder to inspect progress and effort.
Easy to reproduceCopying is nearly free.
Labor-intensiveIntellectual work dominates.
Easy to modifyChanges can hide wider consequences.
Zoom into one property

Easy to modify ≠ easy to change safely

Easy to modifyA local edit may affect requirements, design assumptions, security, tests, data and operations elsewhere in the system.

The code change may be small. The engineering change may not be.

Must Know · continued

Working code is not enough

Something can “work” and still be poor softwareQuality problems may remain hidden behind apparently correct behavior.
Software does not physically wear outIt deteriorates when repeated changes increase complexity or violate the design.
Official Idea, Explained

Copying is cheap.
Creating is not.

The expensive work is discovering what to build.

Then designing, implementing and checking it.

Then coordinating people and changing it safely over time.
