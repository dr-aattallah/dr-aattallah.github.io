Core Software Design Principles

Good design controls complexity and change: divide the problem, separate concerns, hide details, keep responsibilities cohesive, and reduce dependencies.

The core principles

Eight mental hooks

🧩

Divide & conquer

Break a large problem into smaller parts that are easier to understand, assign and change.

🔄

Design for change

Anticipate business, defect, platform, regulatory and quality-driven changes.

🎯

Separation of concerns

Focus on one aspect at a time at both overall-design and component-design levels.

🕶️

Information hiding

Shield implementation details behind stable interfaces to reduce change impact.

🧲

High cohesion

Keep related responsibilities together and unrelated responsibilities out.

🔗

Low coupling

Reduce interdependencies so runtime effects and changes do not propagate unpredictably.

✨

KISS

Favor simple, straightforward, easy-to-understand designs.

🌫️

High abstraction

Hide or defer unnecessary detail so the essence remains understandable.

📌

Encapsulation in the CLO

The supplied CLO names encapsulation, while the detailed slides in this file mainly develop information hiding and private data/stable interfaces rather than a separate encapsulation section.

Cohesion vs coupling

Inside the module vs between modules

🧲 High cohesion

Question: Do the functions inside this module belong together?

Types listed in the source: functional, layer, communicational, sequential, procedural, temporal and utility.

↔

🔗 Low coupling

Question: How strongly does this module depend on other modules?

Types listed: content, common, control, stamp, data, routine call, type use, inclusion/import and external.

Bad design smell

Low cohesion + high coupling

✕ Everything module

Unrelated use cases or methods are grouped together, while many external dependencies leak into the module.

→

✓ Focused module

Responsibilities are related, interfaces are stable, implementation details are hidden and dependencies are controlled.

How systems divide

Decomposition can continue recursively

1System

May divide into clients/servers or subsystems.

2Subsystem

Can divide into packages.

3Package

Divides into classes.

4Class

Divides into methods.

5Goal

Keep each level understandable and changeable.

Memory hook

Cohesion pulls related things together. Coupling measures what ties modules to each other. Information hiding protects the boundary.
