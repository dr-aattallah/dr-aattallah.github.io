Software Engineering vs. Computer Science

Connect quality engineering to the broader engineering trade-offs introduced in Topic 01.

9of 9 pages
HookWhy would a software engineer knowingly choose a solution that is not mathematically optimal?

Because real engineering optimizes under cost, schedule, risk, people, maintainability, and changing requirements—not technical elegance alone.

Learning Focus

Contrast the lecture’s framing of computer science and software engineering, then integrate the major ideas of Topic 02.

Computer Science

  • Pursue optimal solutions.
  • Cost is not the primary consideration.
  • Programming in the small.
  • Technical issues.
  • Dealing with tame problems.
FOUNDATION →

Software Engineering

  • “Good enough” can be enough.
  • Cost matters — PQCT.
  • Programming in the large.
  • All issues and aspects.
  • Dealing with wicked problems.
  • Builds on computer science and other disciplines.
Topic 02 Review

Key Takeaways

  • SQA ensures product/process conformance to requirements and quality standards.
  • Quality cost includes prevention/assurance and failure costs; defects become more expensive when discovered late.
  • Verification: implementation vs. specification. Validation: model/product vs. real need.
  • Static techniques examine artifacts without execution; dynamic testing executes software.
  • Inspection, walkthrough, desk checking, and peer review differ in rigor, process, and purpose.
  • Metrics make attributes measurable, but require interpretation.
  • Quality attributes include usability, efficiency, reliability, maintainability, and reusability.
  • Quality has short-term and long-term dimensions, including scalability and future change.
