Software Engineering vs. Computer Science

Connect quality engineering to the broader engineering trade-offs introduced in Topic 01.

9of 9 pages
HookWhy would a software engineer knowingly choose a solution that is not mathematically optimal?

Because real engineering optimizes under cost, schedule, risk, people, maintainability, and changing requirements—not technical elegance alone.

Learning Focus

Contrast the lecture’s framing of computer science and software engineering, then integrate the major ideas of Topic 02.

Computer Science

  • Pursue optimal solutions.
  • Cost is not the primary consideration.
  • Programming in the small.
  • Technical issues.
  • Dealing with tame problems.

Software Engineering

  • “Good enough” can be enough.
  • Cost matters — PQCT.

  • All issues and aspects.

Topic 02 Review

Key Takeaways

  • SQA ensures product/process conformance to requirements and quality standards.
  • Quality cost includes prevention/assurance and failure costs; defects become more expensive when discovered late.
  • Verification: implementation/work product vs. specification. Validation: product/model vs. real need.
  • Static techniques examine artifacts without execution; dynamic testing executes software.
  • Inspection, walkthrough, desk checking, and peer review differ in rigor, process, and purpose.
  • Metrics make attributes measurable, but require interpretation and context.
  • Quality attributes include usability, efficiency, reliability, maintainability, and reusability.
  • Quality has short-term and long-term dimensions, including scalability and future change.
Whole-topic transfer challenge

Quality-engineer a Mobile Banking Release

A new release adds instant international transfers. Use the entire topic rather than one technique.

Cost of quality

What prevention/appraisal work is worth paying for before release, and what failure costs could appear afterward?

Verification & validation

What evidence shows the implementation follows specifications, and what evidence shows customers can safely accomplish the intended task?

Static & dynamic

What can be reviewed without execution, and what behavior must be executed?

Reviews

Which artifacts deserve peer review or formal inspection before deployment?

Metrics

What measurable indicators could support decisions about defects, complexity, reliability or maintainability?

Attributes over time

Which qualities matter immediately, and which design choices determine whether the feature remains safe and maintainable as regulations and traffic change?

Reveal a model reasoning path

Invest early in requirement/security reviews, design inspection and test planning because post-release financial failures are expensive. Verify transfer limits, authorization rules and audit behavior against specifications; validate that the workflow meets real customer and regulatory needs. Review requirements/design/code statically and execute transaction, failure, load and recovery scenarios dynamically. Use defect and complexity/reliability measures as evidence rather than goals by themselves. Judge immediate correctness/usability together with long-term maintainability, scalability and adaptability.

Exam lens · critical distinctions

Do not confuse the question with the technique

Verification vs. validation

These describe different quality questions; do not reduce them to “static vs. dynamic.” Verification can use static or dynamic evidence depending on what is being checked.

Review vs. testing

A review examines an artifact; testing executes software. Both can uncover defects, but through different mechanisms.

Metric vs. quality

A metric is evidence about an attribute, not the attribute itself and not proof of quality in isolation.

Short-term vs. long-term

A release can work today yet accumulate design choices that make tomorrow's change, scale or maintenance expensive.
