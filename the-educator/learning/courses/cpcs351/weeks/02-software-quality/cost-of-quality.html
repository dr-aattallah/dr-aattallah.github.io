Cost of Quality

Understand why quality spending is an economic engineering decision, not optional overhead.

2of 9 pages
HookWould you rather pay to prevent a defect today—or pay for the same defect after thousands of customers depend on it?

Quality has a cost, but failure has a cost too. Software engineering balances both.

Learning Focus

Explain the two major components of cost of quality and why defect-removal cost rises as defects escape into later life-cycle stages.

Official Lecture Content

Two Sides of the Cost of Quality

Prevention / Assurance Cost

  • Implementing an SQA framework — once.
  • Performing SQA activities — ongoing.
  • Monitoring and improving the framework — ongoing.
  • Equipment, machines, and tools.
⚖

Failure Cost

  • Analyzing and removing failures.
  • Lost productivity for developers and customers.
  • Liability, lawsuits, and higher insurance premiums.
  • Damage to the company’s reputation.

The lecture’s key trade-off is simple: spending nothing on prevention does not make quality free—it shifts spending toward failure.

The original lecture illustrates relative correction cost climbing dramatically—from the order of 1 in early stages toward values as high as 1000 in field operation. The exact multiplier is less important than the engineering lesson: find problems early.

Decide Where to Spend

A team says requirements reviews are “too expensive” and wants to save all quality effort for system testing. What cost are they ignoring?

Self-Feedback: They are ignoring defect propagation. A requirement defect can contaminate design, code, tests, documentation, deployment, and customer operations. Prevention cost is visible early; failure cost is often much larger and arrives later.
