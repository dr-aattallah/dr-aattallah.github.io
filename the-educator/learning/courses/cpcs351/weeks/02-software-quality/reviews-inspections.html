Topic 02 · Lesson 05

Inspections, Walkthroughs & Peer Review

Compare the main static review techniques and understand how peer review works as a process.

5of 9 pages
HookThree people read the same code. One uses test data, one uses a checklist, and one reads alone. Are they doing the same review?

No. The lecture distinguishes static techniques by structure, participants, and purpose.

Learning Focus

Compare inspection, walkthrough, desk checking, and peer review, and explain the peer-review procedure.

Inspection

Uses a checklist or criteria for common errors, standards, and consistency rules. It detects defects rather than correcting them and can examine requirements, design, code, test data, and other representations.

Walkthrough

The developer reads or manually simulates the product, often using simple test data. Participants ask questions, stimulate doubt, and discuss the work. It is relatively informal.

Desk Checking

Code reading by a single person. The lecture describes it as informal and less effective than walkthrough or inspection.

Program Inspection: What Counts as a Defect?

Logical errors, suspicious anomalies such as an uninitialized variable, and non-compliance with standards. The inspector’s goal is detection; correction is a later responsibility.

Peer Review

The product is reviewed by peers using review questions designed to assess it. Reviewers may have one to two weeks to work independently, and results may vary because reviewers differ in knowledge, experience, background, and criticality.

Present a product overview and assign material to reviewers.
Reviewers evaluate independently and answer review questions.
At the meeting, reviewers present comments; the developer answers and clarifies.
The developer fixes problems and summarizes solutions.
Reviewers check changes; a second meeting may be needed.
Choose the Technique

Your goal is consistent detection of known coding-standard violations across a team. Walkthrough or inspection?

Self-Feedback: Inspection is the stronger fit because it explicitly uses criteria/checklists for common errors, standards, and consistency rules.
