Topic 02 · Lesson 01

Software Quality & SQA

Build a complete mental model of software quality assurance before studying its techniques.

1of 9 pages
HookCan software pass every test you wrote and still be a low-quality product?

Yes. Quality is broader than “no observed bugs”: it includes conformance to requirements and standards, fitness for real customer needs, and qualities such as reliability and maintainability.

Learning Outcome · CLO #2

Define software quality and explain the role of quality assurance activities in the software process, then distinguish verification, validation, reviews, testing, metrics, and quality attributes in context.

Must Know

What Is Software Quality Assurance?

Software Quality Assurance (SQA) is a set of activities used to ensure that the software product and/or process conforms to established requirements and quality standards.

The lecture organizes SQA around four recurring mechanisms:

Verification
Are we building the product right?
Validation
Are we building the right product?
Technical Reviews
Examine artifacts without waiting for execution
Testing
Execute software to uncover errors
QUALITY
Product + Process

Think of SQA as a shield built throughout the life cycle—not a final gate added after coding.

The Story of This Topic

We begin with why quality costs money, then learn the two central questions of verification and validation. From there we examine static and dynamic techniques, move into reviews and inspections, quantify quality through metrics, and finally ask what “high quality” actually means now and over the product’s lifetime.

Common Misconception

“SQA = testing.” Testing is one SQA activity. Reviews, verification, validation, standards, measurements, and process-oriented quality work all contribute evidence before and beyond execution.

Classify Before You Continue

A requirement says “The system shall lock an account after five failed logins.” A reviewer checks the design against this statement; later, a tester executes five failed logins. Which is verification and which is testing?

Self-Feedback: Checking the design against the requirement is verification. Executing the product and observing behavior is dynamic testing. Both contribute to quality evidence, but by different mechanisms.
