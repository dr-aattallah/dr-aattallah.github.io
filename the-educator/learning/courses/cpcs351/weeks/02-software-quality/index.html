Software Quality & SQA

Build a complete mental model of software quality assurance before studying its techniques.

1of 9 pages
HookCan software pass every test you wrote and still be a low-quality product?

Yes. Quality is broader than “no observed bugs”: it includes conformance to requirements and standards, fitness for real customer needs, and qualities such as reliability and maintainability.

Learning Outcome · CLO #2

Define software quality and explain the role of quality assurance activities in the software process, then distinguish verification, validation, reviews, testing, metrics, and quality attributes in context.

The chapter asks one continuous question: How do we create credible evidence that the software is correct, useful and sustainable? We invest in prevention and appraisal, inspect artifacts before execution, execute software when behavior must be observed, measure what can be quantified, and judge quality against attributes that matter now and over time.

Must Know

What Is Software Quality Assurance?

Software Quality Assurance (SQA) is a set of activities used to ensure that the software product and/or process conforms to established requirements and quality standards.

The lecture organizes SQA around four recurring mechanisms:

Verification
Are we building the product right?
Validation
Are we building the right product?
Technical Reviews
Examine artifacts without waiting for execution
Testing
Execute software to uncover errors
QUALITY
Product + Process

Think of SQA as a shield built throughout the life cycle—not a final gate added after coding.

Evidence model

Do not collapse these terms into one bucket

Static evidence

Requirements, design and code can be examined without executing the software. Reviews and inspections can therefore find defects early.

Dynamic evidence

Testing requires execution and compares observed behavior with expected behavior or needs.

Verification question

Does the work product conform to its specification, standards or preceding artifacts?

Validation question

Does the resulting product/model satisfy the intended real-world need?

The Story of This Topic

We begin with why quality costs money, then learn the two central questions of verification and validation. From there we examine static and dynamic techniques, move into reviews and inspections, quantify quality through metrics, and finally ask what “high quality” actually means now and over the product’s lifetime.

Common Misconception

“SQA = testing.” Testing is one SQA activity. Reviews, verification, validation, standards, measurements, and process-oriented quality work all contribute evidence before and beyond execution.

Apply it · Online Examination System

A requirement says “After time expires, answers shall be submitted automatically.” A reviewer checks the design against this requirement; later a tester runs an exam until the timer expires; students then report whether automatic submission actually supports the intended examination workflow. Identify the kinds of evidence.

Reveal reasoning: design checking is static verification; executing the timer scenario is dynamic testing and can provide verification evidence against the stated requirement; evaluating whether the behavior serves the real examination need is validation-oriented evidence. The same project needs several kinds of quality evidence.
