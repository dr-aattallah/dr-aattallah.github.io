Short-Term vs. Long-Term Quality

Judge software quality across today’s needs and tomorrow’s change and scale.

8of 9 pages
HookA system satisfies today’s users perfectly. Does that guarantee it is high quality?

Not if tomorrow’s data volume, features, or business needs make it expensive or impossible to evolve.

Learning Focus

Contrast short-term and long-term quality and explain why maintainability, future needs, and scalability matter.

Short-Term Quality

  • Does the software meet the customer’s immediate needs?
  • Is it sufficiently efficient for the volume of data we have today?
→

Long-Term Quality

  • Maintainability.
  • Ability to support the customer’s future needs.
  • Scalability: can it handle larger volumes of data?

The Engineering Tension

“Good enough today” may be rational—but only when future change and growth risks are consciously considered. Overengineering everything is wasteful; ignoring likely evolution creates expensive technical debt. This connects directly to Topic 01’s PQCT trade-offs.

Look Beyond Today

A registration system handles 2,000 students perfectly but cannot scale to 50,000 without a rewrite. Is the current system low quality?

Self-Feedback: Short-term quality may be acceptable, but long-term scalability is weak. The judgment depends on whether growth to 50,000 is a realistic future requirement.
