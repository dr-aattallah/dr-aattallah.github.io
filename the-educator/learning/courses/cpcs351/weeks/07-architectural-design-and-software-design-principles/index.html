From Requirements to Architecture

Requirements tell us what the product must achieve. Architecture makes the first major decisions about how the software will be organized to achieve it.

1of 10 pages
Learning focus

Define software architecture, explain why it matters, and connect requirements, non-functional properties, trade-offs, structure, interfaces, interactions, rationale, and traceability.

The bridge

What → How

1Problem & needs

Business goals, current situation, stakeholder needs.

2Requirements

What the product must do and the constraints it must respect.

3Architecture

Major subsystems/components, interfaces, interactions and style.

4Detailed design

Classes, UI, algorithms, protocols and lower-level behavior.

5Implementation

Construction, integration, deployment and feedback.

What architecture contains

Structure + interaction + rationale

🧩Elements

Subsystems, components and other major building blocks.

🔗Form

How elements connect, interact and compose into a system.

🧭Rationale

Why the design choices were made and which constraints drove them.

🧱Constraints

Rules and non-functional properties that shape the structure.

The lecture presents architecture as high-level structure involving elements, interactions, composition patterns, constraints and design rationale.

Why it matters

Architecture amplifies quality decisions

⚡Performance

Data flow, communication and deployment choices affect speed and efficiency.

🛡️Security

Boundaries and interfaces determine where protection and trust decisions live.

🧰Maintainability

Good decomposition localizes change; poor decomposition spreads it.

♻️Evolution

Architecture is a primary artifact for constructing, managing and evolving the system.

Trade-offs

There is rarely one “best” architecture

⚖️ Typical trade-offs

  • Schedule ↔ Budget
  • Cost ↔ Robustness
  • Fault tolerance ↔ Size
  • Security ↔ Speed

📝 Engineering discipline

Record important decisions and their rationale, and maintain traceability back to the requirements that justify them.

Memory hook

Requirements define the promise. Architecture defines the high-level structure that makes the promise achievable.
