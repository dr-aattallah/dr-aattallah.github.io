Client-Server, Peer-to-Peer & Blackboard

Recognize additional style mappings and understand the detailed client-server example.

10of 14
Learning focus

Explain the source client-server diagram and identify where peer-to-peer and blackboard appear in the chapter.

  • The client-server diagram shows Passenger Check-In Client 1, 2, and 3 communicating with a Flight Information System server.
  • The communication is shown as remote procedure calls.
  • The source identifies UML notation for software components, input/output ports, and user-defined stereotypes such as «client» and «server».
  • Client-server systems map to Client-Server architecture.
  • Distributed, decentralized systems map to Peer-to-Peer architecture.
  • Heuristic problem-solving systems map to Blackboard architecture.
  • The supplied slides develop Client-Server with a detailed diagram, while Peer-to-Peer and Blackboard are listed in the system-style mapping without equivalent detailed diagrams.
«server» Flight Information System
«client» Passenger Check-In 1
«client» Passenger Check-In 2
«client» Passenger Check-In 3
«remote procedure call» through component ports
Remember

Do not invent equal detail for styles the source only names; know the mapping, and know the client-server example deeply.
