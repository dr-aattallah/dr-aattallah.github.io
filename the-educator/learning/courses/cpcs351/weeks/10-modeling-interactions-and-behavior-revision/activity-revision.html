Activity Diagram Revision

Review workflow, conditional routes, parallel work, and the core notation used to express them.

4of 6 pages

What an activity diagram describes

  • Activity diagrams describe the workflow behavior of a system and the sequence of activities performed.
  • They can show activities that are conditional or parallel.
  • They do not provide detail about how objects behave over their lifetime or how objects collaborate.
  • An activity is an ongoing, though interruptible, execution of a workflow step such as an operation or transaction.
  • An activity is represented by a rounded rectangle, and its text should be an activity — normally a present-tense verb phrase.

How to draw the flow

  • Read the diagram from top to bottom.
  • A fork starts multiple activities that occur at the same time.
  • A branch/decision selects activities according to conditions.
  • A merge reunites alternative routes after conditional behavior.
  • A join synchronizes parallel activities before continuing.

Order-processing pattern from the revision

Start → Receive Order → Fork. One route fills the order and branches to Overnight Delivery for a rush order or Regular Delivery otherwise; the alternatives merge. In parallel, another route sends the invoice and receives payment. A join synchronizes the parallel work before Close Order → End.
