ATM Withdrawal — Read the Diagram

Use swimlanes, guards, decisions, fork/join, and merge to explain a complete workflow.

5of 6 pages

Responsibilities / swimlanes

Customer
Insert card, enter PIN/amount, take money, take card.
ATM Machine
Coordinates the interaction, displays information, ejects card.
Bank
Authorize PIN, check balance, debit account.

Trace the withdrawal

The use case begins when the customer inserts the card and enters the PIN. Authorization branches on guards such as [valid PIN] and [invalid PIN]. After an amount is entered, the bank checks the account balance and uses conditions such as [balance ≥ amount] and [balance < amount]. Successful processing coordinates debit and cash-related activities, then joins the required flow, shows the balance where appropriate, merges alternative routes, ejects the card, and ends when the customer takes it.

Added challenge

Suppose the ATM can also print a receipt. Where would you place it? If receipt printing and cash dispensing can occur independently after authorization, model them as parallel activities after a fork and synchronize them with a join before ejecting the card.

Reading strategy

First identify lanes (who), then decisions and guards (which path), then fork/join (what is parallel), and only then trace the complete workflow.
