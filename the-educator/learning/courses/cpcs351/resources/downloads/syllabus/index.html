What you’re here to learn

The foundations needed to develop medium-to-large software systems and communicate engineering decisions clearly.

01 · LEARNING

From problem to system

Move through lifecycle thinking, requirements development, object orientation, architecture and design, then software testing. The emphasis is on how engineering decisions connect.

14

Learning outcomes

Lifecycle, quality, process models, requirements, UML, architecture, patterns, communication and testing.

Requirements

Elicit, analyze and classify requirements, then translate stakeholder goals into precise use cases.

Modeling

Use case, class/domain, sequence, state and activity models to reveal system structure and behavior.

Design

Apply separation of concerns, information hiding, coupling/cohesion, architecture and responsibility patterns.

05 · POLICIES

Stay connected

Check email and Blackboard regularly for announcements, assignments and material updates.

Attendance matters

Attendance is mandatory. Missing more than 25% of lectures results in DN; lab attendance requirements also apply.

Labs are for doing

Use lab time to practice modeling tools, analysis and design. Participation and submission of required lab work are expected.

Academic integrity

Discuss concepts, but do not copy or modify another student’s analysis/design. Submitted project reports are checked for similarity.
