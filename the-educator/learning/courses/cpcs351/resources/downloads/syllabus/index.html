What you’re here to learn

The official syllabus introduces the foundations needed to develop medium-to-large software systems and asks students to apply them to a real-world project.

From problem to system

Move through software lifecycle thinking, requirements development, object orientation, software architecture and design, then software testing. The goal is not memorizing notation; it is learning how engineering decisions connect.

14

Learning outcomes

Lifecycle, quality, process models, requirements, UML, architecture, patterns, communication, and testing.

Requirements

Elicit, analyze, classify functional and non-functional requirements, and translate them into use cases.

Modeling

Use case, class/domain, sequence, state, and activity modeling to reveal structure and behavior.

Design

Apply separation of concerns, information hiding, coupling/cohesion, architecture, and design patterns.

Know the rules. Then focus on learning.

Stay connected

Check email and Blackboard regularly for announcements, assignments, and material updates.

Attendance matters

Attendance is mandatory. Missing more than 25% of lectures results in DN; lab attendance requirements also apply.

Labs are for doing

Use lab time to practice modeling tools, analysis, and design. Participation and submission of lab work are required.

Academic integrity

Discuss concepts, but do not copy or modify another student’s analysis/design. Submitted project reports are checked for similarity.
