Delivery timing, formative feedback and debrief notes for the instructor.

Delivery and formative feedback

Use the short reasoning cycles selectively: choose one two-minute attempt per meeting within the existing retrieval or exit-response allocation. The other attempts support ungraded independent study; do not add seven new in-class activities or submissions. Ask students to revise a sentence using the feedback criteria rather than copy the model.

Instructor timing and preparation guide · three × 50 minutes

Assessment distinction: Foundation Workshop is ungraded. The five questions later on this page are ungraded practice. The official Quick Check 1 is individual and graded in Blackboard. Studio 1: Team Workflow takes place in Unit 02, Week 2; this foundation workshop is not one of the six formal Studios.

Instructor Notes — expected reasoning & debrief
Pause after diagnosis: ask, “Which of these problems could exist even if every feature passed its demo?”

Expected misconception: students may prescribe tools (“use GitHub”, “write documentation”) without linking them to a risk. Push them to explain the causal chain: symptom → engineering weakness → consequence → practice → evidence.

Debrief target: the app did not fail because “the programmers were bad”; it failed as a professional product because change, evidence, shared knowledge, quality and operational responsibility were not engineered.

Calibration: judge the causal chain, not the tool name

Model for E1: confirmed reservations are missing after restart; persistence or environment mismatch is a hypothesis, not a proven defect. Students lose trust and room access. Correlate confirmation IDs with saved records, reproduce the restart, and check that every accepted booking remains retrievable in the intended environment.

Acceptable alternatives: prioritize E2 if change risk is justified, or E3 if recovery exposure is justified. Do not require the same tool or priority from every team.

Fast formative feedback — no marks
CriterionNot yet convincingConvincing evidence
ObservationGeneral complaint or invented factSpecific symptom linked to E1, E2 or E3
ReasoningAssumption stated as proven causePlausible hypothesis plus a check or uncertainty
Impact“Bad quality” without affected peopleNamed stakeholder and concrete consequence
ResponseUnexplained tool prescriptionPractice proportionate to the identified risk
Verification“Test it” or “make it secure”Observable result that would support or challenge the decision

Feedback in one sentence: “Your symptom is supported by E2; strengthen the answer by stating what the regression check must observe.” Sample two teams and use peer feedback for the rest.

M0 readiness: the student can connect a real problem to stakeholders, a dependency, one quality risk and a future evidence check. These are draft hypotheses, not a new graded deliverable or a frozen specification.

Instructor Notes — final misconception check
Ask: “Complete this sentence: Software Engineering = ?”

Misconception to challenge: Software Engineering = Programming + UML + Documentation.

Target message: Software Engineering = making justified engineering decisions about software throughout its life, under real constraints, with evidence.

If students can transfer this reasoning to CampusCare without copying RoomNow's exact solution, Unit 01 has achieved its purpose.
