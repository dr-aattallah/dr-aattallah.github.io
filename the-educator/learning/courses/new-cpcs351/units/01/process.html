Four recurring activities

Sommerville identifies four fundamental activities that appear in all software processes, although different methods organize them differently. This is more useful than memorizing one lifecycle diagram because it exposes the engineering purpose underneath different process models.

▤ Theory
FOUR FUNDAMENTAL ACTIVITIES · NOT NECESSARILY FOUR LINEAR PHASES
SPECIFYWhat should the software do, for whom, and under what constraints?
DEVELOPHow should the solution be designed and implemented?
VALIDATEWhat evidence shows that the software satisfies its intended needs?
EVOLVEHow will the software change as needs, defects and context change?

Do not interpret this as “finish specification forever, then code, then test once.” In iterative development, specification, development and validation can recur in small cycles. Evolution begins as soon as a useful system meets a changing world. Unit 02 will examine process models and Agile workflow in depth; here the goal is to recognize the invariant engineering purposes.

▤ Theory
ITERATIVE VIEW · THE ACTIVITIES FEED ONE ANOTHER
Need & Contextstakeholders · constraints · risks
Specify ↔ Develop ↔ Validaterepeat in useful increments
Evolutionfeedback · defects · new needs · technology change
◇ Example

See how the reasoning improves

Facilities changes the rule so staff may reserve rooms farther in advance than students.

Weak response

“Edit the date limit and deploy.”

Stronger response — and why

Clarify the rule and exceptions, change the implementation, validate both roles and conflicts, then monitor and evolve the behavior. The activities may overlap and repeat.

Carry forward: Revisit the activities needed for the changed rule; they can overlap and recur.
