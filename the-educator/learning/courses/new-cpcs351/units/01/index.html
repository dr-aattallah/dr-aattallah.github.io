Start with the Puzzle

Two teams can write code that produces the same output today. One product survives five years of change; the other collapses after six months. What did the first team engineer that the second team did not?

◇ ExampleSMART CAMPUS · OPENING SCENARIO

The RoomNow problem

KAU wants a campus room-booking service. A student prototype already books an available room. It works in a demo. The university now wants thousands of students and staff to use it, wants access rules, auditability, integration with identity systems, reliable availability during registration periods, and safe evolution when policies change.

Prototype questionCan a user reserve a room?
Engineering questionCan the service remain correct, secure, understandable and changeable when many people depend on it?
Professional questionWho is affected when the system makes a wrong decision, leaks data, or becomes unavailable?
✎ ExerciseTHINK BEFORE READING

What changed when the prototype became a university service?

  1. List three new stakeholders.
  2. List three qualities that now matter beyond “it works.”
  3. Identify one future change that the design must survive.
Reveal a strong line of reasoning
Scale and organizational dependence change the problem. Identity administrators, facilities staff, security teams, support staff and decision makers become stakeholders. Availability, security, privacy, maintainability, usability and auditability matter. The engineering problem is therefore not simply adding features; it is controlling complexity and change while protecting value and people.
