CONCEPT 01

Problem before solution

Requirements engineering begins by understanding the problem, its stakeholders, context and constraints. Do not jump from a feature request straight to implementation.

ROOMNOW EXAMPLERoomNow users ask for 'easy booking'. That phrase is not yet a requirement: easy for whom, in which context and under what constraints?
CONCEPT 02

Stakeholders and viewpoints

Identify users, administrators, operators, security/privacy stakeholders and people affected indirectly. Stakeholders can have conflicting goals; document assumptions and negotiate priorities.

ROOMNOW EXAMPLEStudents want instant booking; facilities staff need room restrictions; IT requires university sign-on; privacy officers limit data retention.
CONCEPT 03

Elicitation and evidence

Use interviews, observation, existing policies, document review, workshops and prototypes where appropriate. Ask open questions, record the source and distinguish observed facts from assumptions.

ROOMNOW EXAMPLEInstead of 'Should booking be fast?', ask 'Walk me through your last room-booking attempt. Where did it fail and why?'
CONCEPT 04

Functional requirements

Functional requirements describe system services and behaviors. State triggers, expected outcomes, relevant conditions and exceptions using unambiguous language.

ROOMNOW EXAMPLEFR-01: The system shall reject a booking request when an active booking already occupies the requested room and overlapping time interval.
CONCEPT 05

Non-functional requirements and constraints

Non-functional requirements specify quality expectations or constraints. Make them measurable where practical; distinguish a quality target from an imposed technology or policy.

ROOMNOW EXAMPLENFR-01: 95% of room-search requests shall complete within two seconds under an agreed test workload. Constraint: authenticate through university SSO.
CONCEPT 06

Validation and conflict resolution

Check correctness, completeness for scope, consistency, feasibility, unambiguity and testability. Validate with stakeholders and record unresolved questions.

ROOMNOW EXAMPLE'Only staff may cancel bookings' conflicts with a user story permitting students to cancel their own bookings. Identify the conflict and request a decision before implementation.
CONCEPT 07

Scope and prioritization

Set the system boundary, define in-scope and out-of-scope capabilities, and rank work by user value, risk and dependencies. A small, coherent initial scope is preferable to a large unverified wish list.

ROOMNOW EXAMPLEM0 is a credible proposal and team setup, not a complete requirements specification. Detailed user stories, use cases and traceability follow in Unit 04.
