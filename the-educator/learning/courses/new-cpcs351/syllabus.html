01 · COURSE OVERVIEW

Practice-centered by design.

The course uses selected models, modern collaborative workflow, testing, quality evidence and responsible engineering judgment around the same evolving product.

Course objective

Students analyze software problems, specify requirements, use selected models, apply fundamental design and architecture principles, collaboratively develop and review changes using a traceable workflow, verify software through testing, and evaluate engineering decisions considering quality, security, privacy, professional responsibility, and responsible use of AI.

Main textbook

Ian Sommerville, Software Engineering, 10th Edition, Global Edition, Pearson. Selected sections are assigned across the semester.

Modern companion

Ian Sommerville, Engineering Software Products: An Introduction to Modern Software Engineering, Global Edition, Pearson. Students are not expected to purchase both books unless instructed.

06 · COURSE POLICIES

Clear expectations for engineering work.

Blackboard remains the official course record. The engineering workspace provides working evidence.

Official communication

Students are responsible for checking Blackboard and official university email for announcements, assessment information and schedule updates.

Attendance

Attendance, DN, absence and make-up examination decisions follow applicable KAU and FCIT regulations.

Traceable teamwork

Students may be asked to explain issues, changes, reviews, tests, design decisions and personal contribution. Commit count alone is not a contribution score.

Responsible AI

When AI is allowed, students remain responsible for correctness, security, privacy, understanding, disclosure and justification. Expected pattern: Generate → Critique → Verify → Justify.
