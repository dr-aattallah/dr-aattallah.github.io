CPCS351 Instructor Delivery Guide

Course identity: From Programmer to Software Engineer. This online operational guide reflects the proposed QA-aligned schedule. Blackboard and the formally approved syllabus remain authoritative for actual semester dates.

Assessment (100 marks): Semester Engineering Project 35 (M0 5, M1 7, M2 8, M3 10, Final Release 5); three individual Challenges 15 (5 each); five individual Quick Checks 10 (2 each); Midterm 20; Final 20. Studios are formative. Final exam occurs outside the 45 instructional meetings.

Activity protocol

Studios: six in W2, W4, W6, W9, W11 and W13. Brief in the preceding meeting (usually final 5–10 minutes), verify student access, then use the activity meeting for a 5-minute recap, short demonstration, 20–30 minutes of team practice and a debrief/project transfer. No separate studio marks.

Challenges: three individual graded tasks in W5 M3, W10 M1 and W14 M1. Brief the rubric and response format in advance, not the unseen challenge. Identify → Analyze → Improve → Verify → Justify.

Quick Checks: QC1 W1, QC2 W3, QC3 W7, QC4 W10, QC5 W12. Graded individually in Blackboard. In-unit website practice is ungraded.

Project: introduce each milestone 1–2 weeks ahead, use existing Studios and clinics to produce evidence, and collect stable links via Blackboard. M0 W3, M1 W7, M2 W10, M3 W14, Final W15.

Studio implementation checklist

  1. Confirm every student can access the repository and project board before Studio 1.
  2. State scenario, expected artifact, roles, timebox and how it feeds the current project milestone.
  3. Demonstrate one complete minimal example; avoid spending the whole meeting on configuration.
  4. Provide a fallback team workspace if accounts or connectivity fail.
  5. During practice, inspect evidence rather than only asking whether the team is finished.
  6. Close with common mistakes, an improvement and the project transfer.

Depth boundaries and grading integrity

Do not teach every UML notation, pattern catalog, Scrum ceremony or Git command. Focus on competencies and credible engineering evidence. Protect independent Challenge assessment from collaborative Studio coaching. Grade contributions by traceable work and explanations, not raw commit counts. For AI-assisted work require human critique and verification; never use real personal data in public AI tools.
